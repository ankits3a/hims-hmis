HMIS staff app: privacy policy

Last updated 23 October 2026

HMIS is the hospital's own information system. The HMIS staff app is for the hospital's staff only. You need an account from the hospital to sign in. This page says what the app collects, why, where it is kept and who can see it.

Patient information

The app shows and records patient information as part of the hospital's medical records. The hospital is responsible for this information under Indian law, including the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Staff see a patient's information only when their job needs it.

What the app uses, and why

  • Your account. Your name, username and job in the hospital. The app keeps your sign-in on the phone in the phone's secure storage. Fingerprint or face unlock is checked by the phone itself. HMIS never receives it.
  • Location, for attendance only. When you tap Mark attendance, the app asks for your location and reads it once, never in the background. The hospital's server works out whether you are inside or outside the hospital premises, and how many metres away you are. It saves only that result. Your exact coordinates are never stored. If you do not allow location, the mark is saved as "location not shared". An app mark does not change the attendance machine's record.
  • Aadhaar, if you enter it. It links you to the attendance machine. The hospital keeps it only as a code, plus the Aadhaar's last four digits. The full number is not stored.
  • Camera. The camera opens only when you open it. It is used to scan a barcode or QR code (for example on a patient card), to photograph paper prescription slips, and to photograph a lab analyser's printed slip. You can also pick one photo of a lab slip from your gallery. The app does not ask for access to your photo library. Photos go to the hospital's servers.
  • Microphone, for voice notes. A doctor can record a spoken note during a consultation. The recording goes through the hospital's server to an outside speech-to-text service, which turns it into text. The audio is not kept. The patient's name is not sent with it, but anything said aloud is in the recording. The text becomes part of the consultation note.
  • Lab slip reading. A photo of a lab analyser's slip is sent to an outside AI service to read the results into a draft report. The slip may show the patient's name and hospital number. The service is used on paid terms under which the provider does not use it to train its models. A staff member checks the draft before it is saved.
  • Copilot (optional). The copilot is off until you turn it on in Settings. Some questions go to an outside AI service, only to work out which action you mean. Patient names and numbers are replaced before a question is sent. Every action waits for your tap.
  • Notifications. Notifications carry no patient details: only a short notice and which screen to open. They reach your phone through the phone's notification service. You can turn each kind of notification off in the app.

What stays on your phone

Your sign-in and a short copy of your home screen are kept in the phone's secure storage, so the app opens fast. Logging out clears them. On Android the app blocks screenshots. On iPhone it hides its screen in the app switcher.

Where information is kept

Everything the app records is kept on the hospital's HMIS servers. The outside services named above receive only what is described there. The app has no advertising, no tracking and no outside analytics. Information is not sold.

Who can see it

  • Staff see what their role allows, and nothing more.
  • Every change to a record is logged with the staff member who made it.
  • Attendance: you see your own. Your unit head sees your team's. The Medical Superintendent and the Attendance Committee see everyone's.

How long it is kept

  • Patient records are kept as part of the hospital's medical records.
  • Copilot questions are deleted after 180 days.
  • A lab slip photo that was not used in a report is deleted after 90 days. A photo used in a report is kept with that report.

Your choices

You can refuse location, camera or microphone access in your phone's settings. The rest of the app still works. Only the feature that needs that access stops.

Contact

For any question about this policy or your information, contact the hospital's IT office. If this policy changes, this page will be updated and the date above will change.
