## 5. What changed since the Plan 10 roadmap entry was written

The entry is from 2026-08-20, before 08.5 ran. Four things it says are now wrong or incomplete.

### 5.1 THE ROADMAP SAYS "outbound message queue (pg-boss)". **pg-boss IS DEAD.**

It lost 08.5's FORK-B **by execution, not by preference**: `pg-boss@12.27.0` is ESM-only and the
repo's jest harness cannot parse it — `SyntaxError: Cannot use import statement outside a module`,
**0 tests run**, with no change to `jest.config` or `test/helpers/db.ts`. That fired D2's binding
decision rule. Independently, its cron was measured minute-granular even when handed a 6-field
seconds cron (1 fire in 90 s when asked for 90). It is in **no `package.json` and no lockfile**.

The roadmap's own next sentence is the one that survived: **"The outbound queue runs on 08.5's
worker/scheduler — no second scheduler, no second process."** Design the queue on the shipped
`Scheduler`. If you believe it genuinely needs a job runner, that is a FORK with a decision rule
and a spike question — never an assumption, and never a second process.

### 5.2 08.5 shipped the surfaces you build on. Transcribe these, do not re-derive them

- `Scheduler` — `apps/core/src/kernel/worker/scheduler.ts`: `register(spec: JobSpec)` where
  `JobSpec` is `EverySpec | DailyIstSpec`, `start()`, `stop()` (awaits in-flight runs **and**
  latches `stopped` so nothing starts afterwards), `jobs()`. `JobRun = (now: Date) => Promise<void>`.
  Locks behind an injectable `Locks` (`pgLocks(pool)` in production).
- `registerAllJobs(scheduler, db, registry, consumers: Record<string, Handler>, intervals: JobIntervals)`
  — `apps/core/src/kernel/worker/jobs.ts:92`. **It reads no environment; config arrives as a
  parameter.** Keep it that way (§2.56).
- **Amendment 6's seam, and it binds you:** a new dispatcher consumer needs BOTH its manifest
  declaring the subscription AND its handler in the `consumers` map. **A declared subscription with
  no matching handler is a BOOT ERROR by design.** Half the edit ships a worker that throws at
  startup behind a green suite. Whatever task adds a consumer must own **both** files.
- `alertsConsumer(db): Handler` and `ALERTS_CONSUMER = "kernel.alerts"` —
  `apps/core/src/kernel/alerts/consumer.ts:13,43`. **This is your template for a second consumer**,
  including its idempotency shape.
- `runDispatchCycle(db, bus, opts?: { batchSize?, lookback?, maxAttempts?, now? })` —
  **at-least-once with dedupe.** The claim is recorded **on success**, deliberately the opposite of
  billing's claim-before-work, because the dispatcher's nightmare is losing an event. **Your
  consumer must be idempotent; that is the contract every consumer inherits.**
- **A throwing consumer blocks its own queue for ~30 s before it parks** (backoff
  `min(2^attempts, 60) s`, parking at 5 attempts). An e2e that fires one cycle and expects an
  effect will silently get zero if the handler threw once.
- `alerts` table, `alert.raised` / `alert.read` events, the per-user realtime topic space with
  `authorize`, and `scheduler_heartbeats` — all shipped, all in migration `0014`.

### 5.3 A NEW CONSUMER REPLAYS EVENT HISTORY ON ITS FIRST CYCLE. This is now YOUR problem

`event_cursors.last_seq` defaults to `0` and `runDispatchCycle` creates the row on first sight, so
the first cycle after a consumer is registered reads `seq > max(0 − lookback, 0) = 0` and walks
**every matching event ever recorded**, at 100 per tick. For the alerts consumer that was booked on
Plan 11 as a deployment step, because 08.5 shipped no production deployment.

**Plan 10 adds a consumer to a system that by then has history.** If your notifications consumer
subscribes to anything already flowing, it will attempt to notify on the entire backlog the moment
it is wired — and unlike alerts (which dedupe on `(source_event_id, user_id)` and merely make rows),
**this one sends messages to real people.** Rule on it explicitly.

### 5.4 Two residuals from 08.5, both live

1. **`apps/core/src/worker.ts`'s half of the amendment-6 wire is not regression-guarded.** The file
   calls `bootstrap()` at import time, so nothing can import it, and the e2e re-types the consumers
   expression rather than reading production's. **Deleting a consumer entry would leave the whole
   suite green.** If Plan 10 adds a consumer, it inherits this gap — consider closing it (extract

## 6. Rulings to reach in the brainstorm — do not design around these

### 6.1 IS THE PUBLIC READ SURFACE IN THIS PLAN AT ALL? It may be blocked

The roadmap gives Plan 10 two halves — the **notifications gateway** and the **public read surface**
(`apps/relay`, signed short-lived tokens, queue position, document verification). The second one
depends on a decision the spec lists as a **PRE-GO-LIVE GATE and does not consider made**:

> *"public read-only surface decision — DMZ vs cloud relay (E-1)"* — spec line 793.

It is also entangled with the **deployment topology + second-server decision**, which the roadmap
marks *"decide now"* and which already blocks Plan 11. **Ask the owner directly.** Three honest
shapes, and the brainstorm picks one:

- **(a) Split** — Plan 10 is the gateway; the relay becomes 10.5 or joins 11, where topology lives.
- **(b) Fork-open** — one plan, the relay's tasks designed against both DMZ and cloud-relay with a
  decision rule, as 08.5 did with its scheduler.
- **(c) Both halves now**, if the owner rules E-1 in the brainstorm.

**Do not silently pick (a) by scoping the relay out.** ≤ 6 tasks is a real constraint and the
gateway alone may fill it, but dropping half a roadmap entry is an owner ruling, not an editorial one.

### 6.2 THERE IS NO PROVIDER, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE

Provider selection is **deferred (§19)**. The roadmap's own answer is a **provider-agnostic adapter
plus a dev/console provider**. So Plan 10 ships a gateway that cannot actually deliver a WhatsApp
message, and its tests assert against a console implementation. **Say this out loud in the plan** —
otherwise a reader (or a later gate) will treat the absence of real delivery as an incomplete plan.

Two consequences to rule on: **what does `notification.sent` MEAN when the provider is a console
sink** (a real event, or a lie the golden suite will later have to unpick?); and **where do provider
credentials live** when they arrive — noting D9's precedent that new config keys default in the zod
schema so no `.env` change is needed, and noting that **the repo is public right now**.

### 6.3 THE PATIENT MAY HAVE NO PHONE. This is a designed path, not an edge case

`patients.phone` is **NULLABLE** — `apps/core/src/kernel/db/schema/patients.ts:43`, commented
*"normalized 10-digit Indian mobile; NULLABLE — phoneless patients are a designed path (D-34)"*.
The §11.5 fallback ladder is **WhatsApp → SMS → IVR → manual-notify desk flag**, and that last rung
exists precisely for this. A gateway that assumes a phone number will fail on real patients.

`patients.language` **exists** — `:52`, `'hi' | 'en'`, default `'hi'`, commented *"outbound-message
language (§6), NOT the UI language"*. That is the field template rendering keys on.

### 6.4 THERE IS NO PROMOTIONAL OPT-IN FIELD YET

§11.13's DPDP rule is **transactional flows always, promotional strictly opt-in, captured at
registration, revocable**. No such field exists on `patients` today (the `authorityConsents` /
`consentNote` columns are D-31 guardianship, a different thing). So Plan 10 either adds it —
migration `0015`, plus a registration-screen change, which is scope — or rules that promotional
messages are out of scope until a later plan and ships **only** the transactional path. **Rule it.**

### 6.5 QUIET HOURS, AND WHAT IGNORES THEM

Quiet hours are **9 p.m.–8 a.m.** for non-urgent patient messages; **urgent recalls ignore them by
design**; and 08.5's D12 already ruled that **in-app alerts are exempt** because they do not
interrupt. §11.13's anti-alarm-fatigue rule says **only the active-alert list interrupts; everything
else batches into shift digests.** Decide where that boundary is enforced — per template, per
event, or per channel — and make it one place, not three.

## 7. The four plan-authoring lessons earned in the last two weeks. Apply all of them

These are not general advice. Each was paid for, and three of the four bit Plan 08.5 specifically.

### 7.1 §2.46 — RESOLVE EVERY PATH IN YOUR FILE STRUCTURE AGAINST THE ACTUAL TREE

08.5's compile found **five** paths that did not resolve, and every one would have HALTED a task,
because the frozen-path block is generated from the Files lists — a file the list does not name is
frozen, so the correct action and the forbidden action become the same action. Specimens: it named
`lib/i18n.ts` for strings that live in `locales/*.json`; it named `apps/core/README.md`, which does
not exist; it named a function's test file nowhere. **Modify-targets must exist; create-targets must
not. Check with `test -e`, not by reading.** A plan can be exact about the code it quotes and wrong
about where the code lives — those are two different acts of attention.

### 7.2 §2.47 — LOOK FOR FORWARD REFERENCES BETWEEN YOUR OWN TASKS

08.5's D1 and T2 told wave-2 T2 to import wave-4 T4's exports. Unbuildable as written, and T4 could
not fix it either because the file was frozen to it. **The blindness is structural:** you are
describing the finished system, and the contradiction only exists in the join between the Files
lists and the wave numbers. **The cheap resolution: the earlier task ships the SEAM, the later task
FILLS it** — and the later task's Files list grows by one line. Plan 10 is at high risk here,
because a queue, a consumer, an adapter and a template registry are four things that reference each
other.

### 7.3 §2.49 — AN ASSERTION ABOUT AN UNUSED SEAM IS VACUOUS BY CONSTRUCTION

08.5's spike proved the manifest-subscriptions seam was not merely unbound but **empty** — every
shipped manifest declared `subscriptions: []`. Its planned reconciliation assertion was therefore
literally `[] === []`, which passes forever, and a gate and a checker both waved it through. **The
fix is not to delete such an assertion but to give it a leg that can fail** — a synthetic fixture
that exercises the property — keeping the real one as an honestly-labelled regression pin.

### 7.4 §2.57 — YOUR ASSERTION BOOK'S "EXACT DISCRIMINATING INPUT" IS A PREDICTION, AND ONE WAS WRONG

08.5's row L14 named an instant it claimed would separate an IST-correct scheduler from a
UTC-mutant one. Executed: **both passed.** The mutant survived the census AND survived the Book's
own stated input, because a `>=` comparison made the boundary behave differently than predicted.
Rule 21 says never claim an assertion discriminates unless you built the mutant and watched it fail;
**this extends it to the INPUT a plan writes down.** Where you cannot be certain an input separates
two implementations, say so in the row and mark it for the task to confirm by execution.

## 8. Traps specific to this plan

1. **No second scheduler, no second process** for the queue (roadmap trap, 08.5 Global Constraint 4).
   The relay, if it ships, is a *separate deployable* — that is not the same thing as a second
   scheduler, and the plan should say why.
2. **The relay holds no secrets and no DB.** It is a cache of pushed snippets keyed by token. Short
   TTLs, revocable on supersession, **no PHI beyond the token**, and **no inbound path** to core.
3. **Messages are irreversible.** Unlike an alert row, a sent WhatsApp message cannot be un-sent.
   That pushes the send path toward CRITICAL tier, and it makes §5.3's backlog replay a
   people-facing hazard rather than a volume one.
4. **A new dispatcher consumer inherits at-least-once delivery.** Idempotency is the consumer's
   job. For notifications that means a dedupe key that survives redelivery — decide what it is.
5. **`tsx` and class-typed injection.** If the relay is a new entrypoint run under `tsx`, every
   provider must be injected by token; esbuild emits no `design:paramtypes` and Nest will silently
   inject `undefined`. This is measured, not theoretical — it is why `pnpm start:dev` is currently
   broken for the API (a pre-existing Plan 07 defect, still open).
6. **Do not touch `dispatcher.ts`'s cursor semantics** to solve §5.3. That was ruled out in 08.5
   and booked on Plan 11; if Plan 10 needs different behaviour, that is an owner ruling.

## 9. Hard constraints

- **≤ 6 tasks, one pipeline.** A seventh task means cutting scope, never adding a task. If both

- The plan is **design law for the pipeline that executes it**. Everything an agent needs must be

- Every clock-reading service takes `now: Date = new Date()` (08.5 Global Constraint 9), and no

- **You do not compile and you do not execute.** End at the committed plan.

## 10. The one thing to keep in view

08.5 switched the operating system on: the sweeps run, the ladder fires, escalations become alert
rows, and a bell in the Shell puts one in front of a human being. **Everything it delivers stops at
the edge of the building.** Plan 10 is where the system first speaks to someone who is not sitting
at a workstation — a patient waiting for a token, a doctor who is off-shift, an owner at 8 a.m.

That is also where it first becomes possible to do visible harm at scale. An alert row that should
not exist is a row; a WhatsApp message that should not exist is a message to a real person, and on
this surface the worst case is messaging the family of a patient who has died. Weight the tiering
accordingly, and treat the suppression rules as the load-bearing ones rather than the plumbing.
