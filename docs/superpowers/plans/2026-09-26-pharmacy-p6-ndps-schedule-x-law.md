# Pharmacy P6: the law for narcotic, psychotropic and Schedule X drugs

**Owner ruling, 2026-09-26.** The hospital pharmacy stocks NDPS narcotic drugs and Schedule X drugs. The owner
named morphine, fentanyl and tramadol injections as examples, and asked for "their law, properly".

**This brief is the research that came first: what the law is, where it says so, and what we build for it.**
The law is quoted from the texts listed below, and the table in §2 cites each one. Where a text could not be
read, or two texts disagree, this brief says so and takes the stricter reading.

## 1. What the law requires

### Essential narcotic drugs: NDPS Act 1985, and NDPS Rules Chapters VA and VB

G.S.R. 359(E) of 5 May 2015 inserted these chapters. Two corrections to the brief as first framed:

- The rules are **Chapter VA (r.52A–52M)** and **Chapter VB (r.52N–52ZA)**. There is no "Chapter VIA".
- **Form 3H is the daily register**, not the annual return. The annual return is **Form 3-I**.

1. **The six essential narcotic drugs.** An *essential narcotic drug* is a narcotic drug the Centre notifies for
   medical and scientific use (s.2(viiia)). S.O. 1181(E) notifies six:
   - codeine and ethylmorphine;
   - fentanyl;
   - hydrocodone;
   - methadone;
   - morphine;
   - oxycodone.

   Two kinds of preparation are excluded:
   - codeine compounded at ≤100 mg per dosage unit and ≤2.5% in undivided preparations;
   - morphine preparations at 0.2% or less.

   Pethidine is **not** on the list. It stays a "manufactured drug" under the State's NDPS rules (s.10 as amended).
   Tramadol is a **psychotropic substance** (Schedule entry 110Y, S.O. 1761(E), 2018), and is also D&C Schedule H1.
2. **The hospital must be a Recognised Medical Institution (RMI) before it possesses or dispenses one.**
   - The State Drugs Controller (the "Controller of Drugs", r.2(da)) recognises it. The application is Form 3F and
     the certificate is Form 3G (r.52-O).
   - The certificate is valid for **at most three years**. Renewal is applied for **at least 60 days before
     expiry**.
   - The RMI **designates a trained registered medical practitioner (RMP)** as the one in charge overall. That name
     is endorsed on Form 3G, and a change is reported within 7 days (r.52Q).
   - Only Government, municipal and Zilla Parishad hospitals are *deemed* RMIs (r.52N). A private hospital applies.
3. **Only a trained RMP prescribes an essential narcotic drug.** Under r.2(ib), the RMP for these chapters is one
   who "has undergone training in pain relief and palliative care … or … opioid substitution therapy".
4. **The prescription (r.52G)** must be:
   - in writing, dated and signed;
   - carrying the prescriber's **full name, address and registration number**;
   - naming the **patient and the patient's address**;
   - stating the **total quantity with the daily dose and the period**;
   - never for the prescriber's own use.
5. **The RMI registers each patient** (r.52R(1)(a)). The record per patient is **Form 3E**:
   - registration number and date;
   - name, and address with contact number;
   - a brief description of the illness;
   - whether the patient is registered with any other RMP or RMI;
   - a table of date, drug, quantity, the patient's signature or thumb impression, and remarks.
6. **The daily register (Form 3H, r.52R(1)(c))** is kept separately for each drug. For each day it records:
   - opening stock;
   - quantity received, from whom, and against which consignment note, bill or invoice;
   - quantity dispensed, with the Form 3E registration number of each patient and the quantity each received;
   - closing stock.

   It is written up for each day before the day closes, on serially numbered pages, and the overall in-charge
   signs it.
7. **Records are kept for two years from the last entry** (r.52R(1)(b)–(c), r.52X). Consignment notes and
   invoices are also kept two years (r.52D(3)). Nothing in the law requires deletion afterwards.
8. **Destruction.** Expired stock is destroyed "**in the presence of an officer nominated by the Controller of
   Drugs**" (r.52V(1)). CAHO's guidance says a hospital official instead; the rule is followed here.
9. **Patient returns are receipts** (r.52V(2)).
10. **No transfer, loan or sale to another institution without the Controller's prior approval** (r.52V(3)).
11. **Returns to the Controller:**
    - an **annual return in Form 3-I** by 31 March for the previous calendar year (r.52R(1)(d));
    - an **estimate in Form 3J** by 30 November, revised by 31 August (r.52T).

    Holdings are capped at the estimate (r.52U).
12. **Storage.** Chapters VA and VB contain **no storage clause**. The NDPS Rules do not say "lock and key". The
    **double lock** (two keys, two people) is **hospital practice and accreditation guidance (CAHO), not law**.
    It is built anyway, as the stricter design.

### Psychotropic substances: NDPS Rules r.65A–67

13. Sale, purchase and use follow the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules (r.65A).
14. A hospital keeps "**proper accounts and records in relation to the purchase and consumption**" (r.66(3)).
    **No form and no retention period are prescribed.** Consignment notes and invoices are kept two years
    (r.67(4)). Private hospitals are licensed under the D&C Rules (r.66(1)).

### Schedule X: Drugs and Cosmetics Rules 1945

15. **The licence.** Schedule X is sold by retail only under a licence in **Form 20F**. Form 20G is wholesale
    (r.61(3)). The application is Form 19C (r.59(2)), and Form 20F is granted only to a pharmacy (r.64 proviso).
    - Since G.S.R. 1337(E) of 2017, the licence is **perpetual while a retention fee is paid every five years**
      (r.63). A late fee of 2% a month runs for six months, and after that the licence is deemed cancelled.
    - A private hospital pharmacy is not exempt. Schedule K item 5A exempts only Government and local-body
      hospitals.
16. **The prescription** is in writing, signed, dated, and names the patient, the patient's address, and the total
    amount and dose (r.65(10)).
    - Schedule X's is **in duplicate, and the licensee keeps one copy for two years** (r.65(9)(a)).
    - It is dispensed **once** unless the prescriber writes otherwise (r.65(11)(a)).
    - The seller writes their **name, address and the date** on it (r.65(11)(c)).
    - **No substitution** (r.65(11A)).
17. **The Schedule X register** (r.65(21)) is bound, serially page-numbered, with separate pages for each drug. It
    records:
    - the date;
    - quantity received, with the supplier's name, address and licence number;
    - the drug name, quantity supplied, manufacturer and batch;
    - the patient's or purchaser's name and address;
    - the prescription reference;
    - the bill number and date;
    - the supervising pharmacist's signature.

    It is kept **two years from the last entry** (r.65(7)).
18. **Storage: "under lock and key in a cupboard or drawer reserved solely for" Schedule X drugs**, or in a
    separate part of the premises that only responsible persons can enter (r.65(12)). This is the only statutory
    lock-and-key rule in either code.
19. **For comparison, the Schedule H1 register** (r.65(3)(1)(h)) holds the prescriber, the patient, the drug and
    the quantity, and is kept **three years**. It already exists (`pharmacy_reg_h1`).

**The Schedule X drugs** (as listed in the CDSCO compilation):

| Group | Drugs |
|---|---|
| Barbiturates | amobarbital, barbital, cyclobarbital, pentobarbital, secobarbital, methylphenobarbital |
| Stimulants | amphetamine, dexamphetamine, methamphetamine, methylphenidate, phenmetrazine |
| Others | glutethimide, phencyclidine, ethchlorvynol, meprobamate |
| Added 2013 | ketamine HCl (G.S.R. 724(E), 2013) |

Morphine and fentanyl are **not** Schedule X. They are NDPS drugs and carry D&C Schedule H.

**Not confirmed, and the reading taken:**

- **G.S.R. 359(E) itself was not read.** Two compilations agree on Chapters VA and VB. An amendment after 2021
  cannot be ruled out. CBN's notification list shows none; its newest rule amendment, G.S.R. 889(E) of 2025,
  touches Forms 4A and 5 only.
- **Codeine's and morphine's exclusions depend on concentration,** which the catalogue does not record reliably.
  *Stricter reading:* every codeine and morphine product is treated as an essential narcotic drug until a
  pharmacist rules a product exempt. The ruling mechanism is deferred (§4).
- **Pethidine** falls under State NDPS rules, which were not checked. *Stricter reading:* it is classed narcotic
  and handled like an essential narcotic drug.
- **Which words G.S.R. 812(E) of 1994 removed from Schedule K item 5A** could not be established. *Stricter
  reading:* a private hospital needs Form 20F.
- **Double lock is not law.** It is built because the owner asked for custody, and because it is the accredited
  practice.
- **Retention.** The law sets minimums: two years for narcotic and Schedule X records, three years for H1. The
  registers are append-only and are never purged.

## 3. What we build for it (P6, law part)

The pattern is the house one: the agent drafts, people confirm. Keys come first. Everything happens inside the
pharmacy desk and the pharmacy office.

1. **Licences as data** (`pharmacy_controlled_licences`). Each is recorded on screen as printed: kind, licence
   number, form, issuing authority, holder, responsible person, valid from, valid until, and where the original is
   kept.

   | Kind | Form | Valid until |
   |---|---|---|
   | RMI recognition | Form 3G, from the State Drugs Controller | the certificate's end |
   | Schedule X retail licence | Form 20F | the date the retention fee falls due |

   - A renewal is a new row.
   - **The doctors trained under r.2(ib)** are recorded beside the RMI licence (`pharmacy_end_prescribers`).
   - The census holds one RED row per licence, one for "a trained prescriber recorded", and one for "a custodian
     pair held".
   - **No current licence means the existing refusal stays** (`schedule_x_not_dispensed_here`, and
     `ndps_not_dispensed_here` for narcotic drugs). Its prose names the missing licence.
2. **Classification.**
   - `formulary_salts.ndps_class` (`narcotic` | `psychotropic`) is written from the cited list in
     `modules/formulary/ndps.ts`:
     - the six essential narcotic drugs, ethylmorphine included, as `narcotic`;
     - pethidine as `narcotic`, on the stricter reading;
     - tramadol as `psychotropic`.
   - A medicine takes the strictest class of its moieties. Schedule X stays the existing
     `formulary_medicines.schedule_flag = 'X'`.
   - `classify-ndps` runs as a dry run first. It reports every catalogue moiety that looks controlled but is not
     on the list: the benzodiazepines, buprenorphine, pentazocine, ketamine and others. **A pharmacist decides
     each one; nothing is guessed.**
   - A **controlled** line is one that is Schedule X or carries an NDPS class.
3. **Custody: the double lock.**
   - Controlled stock lives in **`PHARM-NDPS`**, a store whose attributes say `controlled: true`. It is seeded,
     and it is where Schedule X is kept "under lock and key" (r.65(12)).
   - **Every movement into or out of it** takes two people, the holder and a witness, both recorded
     (`stock_ledger.witness_id`). This covers the GRN posted into it, a transfer received into it, the dispense,
     a return to a supplier, a destruction, and a count's adjustment. The ledger's single writer refuses anything
     else.
   - A narcotic-cabinet item may never enter an open store. The cabinet takes nothing else, because r.65(12)
     says "reserved solely".
   - **The daily balance check:** the holder and a witness count every batch. A variance becomes a
     `materials_stock_adjustment` request to the **medical superintendent**, the existing path.
4. **Dispensing at the desk.** A controlled line is picked from the cabinet. At hand-over the server asks for all
   of the following, and the desk's agent card lists what is still missing:
   - a current licence for the line's class (and, for a narcotic drug, a prescriber on the trained list);
   - the **prescriber's registration number** on the prescription;
   - the **patient's address**;
   - the quantity **computable from the dose, frequency and days, and no more than it**;
   - the **retained prescription**: a `patient_documents` photo or scan, the duplicate for Schedule X;
   - for Schedule X, the pharmacist's word that the **prescription was endorsed** with the seller's name, address
     and date;
   - **who collected it**: the patient, or an attendant with the relation, and the identity document shown;
   - a registered pharmacist;
   - **a second person's witness sign-off**: their username and PIN, which is throttled, and never the holder.

   Substitution of a Schedule X line is refused (r.65(11A)). Each issue writes a register row.
5. **Registers: `controlled_stock_register`, append-only by database trigger** (the `pharmacy_reg_h1` shape).
   - `postMovements` writes one row per cabinet movement **in the same transaction as the ledger row**. The row
     carries the running batch balance and every particular of r.65(21) and Forms 3H and 3E: supplier, supplier
     licence, invoice, prescription reference, bill number and date, patient name and address, prescriber
     registration number, both keys, and the collector.
   - **Prints (A4):**
     - Form 3H by drug and day;
     - the Schedule X register by drug;
     - Form 3E by patient.
   - **The balance view,** per drug per batch over any range: opening + received − issued − destroyed ±
     adjusted = closing. It is **reconciled against the stock ledger** and the identity is asserted in tests. Over
     a calendar year it gives Form 3-I's figures.
6. **Destruction goes through P4's write-off.**
   - At the cabinet it needs the holder and a witness.
   - For NDPS lines it also needs **the Controller-nominated officer's name, designation and nomination order**
     (r.52V(1)), recorded on each register row.
   - A narcotic drug's return to a supplier needs the **Controller's prior-approval reference** (r.52V(3)).
7. **The agent.** The desk's card flags a controlled line and lists what is missing before hand-over. The
   office's "needs you today" lists three things:
   - licences expiring within 60 days (r.52-O's renewal window);
   - whether today's balance check has been done;
   - open discrepancies.
8. **Permissions** (defaults the owner may change), with seed-roles, their counts and the README prose:

   | Permission | Granted to |
   |---|---|
   | `pharmacy.ndps.custody` | `pharmacy_incharge`, `pharmacy` |
   | `pharmacy.ndps.witness` | `pharmacy`, `pharmacy_incharge`, `medical_superintendent`, `materials_head` |
   | `pharmacy.licences.manage` | `pharmacy_incharge`, `owner`, `medical_superintendent` |

   An event is written for every act. There is one migration.

## 4. What the owner must still file, enter or decide

**Filing (law):**

1. **Apply for RMI recognition** in Form 3F to the State Drugs Controller, unless it is already held. Enter the
   **Form 3G** number, the dates and the **designated RMP** at `/pharmacy/office` → Controlled → L.
2. **Hold a Form 20F licence** for the pharmacy (applied for in Form 19C), and enter it with the date its
   **retention fee** falls due.
3. **Record each doctor trained in pain relief and palliative care, or opioid substitution therapy** (r.2(ib)).
   Until one is recorded, narcotic lines are refused.
4. **File the Form 3J estimate** by 30 November and the **Form 3-I annual return** by 31 March. The balance view
   over the year gives 3-I's figures. The estimate is the owner's.

**Running the system:**

5. **Run `classify-ndps --apply`** after reading its dry run. Decide the moieties it reports as unknown.
6. Move every existing controlled stock **into `PHARM-NDPS` under two keys**.
7. **Name the custodians and witnesses** by role.
8. For each destruction, **obtain the Controller's nominee** before posting it.

**Decided, owner may change:**

- Tramadol is under the cabinet regime (the owner named it), although the law asks only for accounts (r.66(3)).
  Other psychotropics stay unclassified until a pharmacist rules.
- Every codeine and morphine product is treated as an essential narcotic drug until a product-level exemption
  exists.
- The patient's UHID is the Form 3E registration number.
- A patient's return of a controlled drug stays refused at the counter. The law would book it as a receipt
  (r.52V(2)); it is deferred.

**Deferred:**

- **Exemptions for low-strength codeine and morphine products,** set per product.
- **Capturing the patient's signature or thumb impression** in Form 3E. The printed form carries the column to
  sign on paper.
- **Enforcing the r.52U holding cap** against the estimate.
- **Home-care issue and return** (r.52W).
- **Keeping a scanned copy of each licence** in the app. Today only a reference to where the original is kept is
  recorded.
- **Walk-in sale of Schedule X** at `PHARM-RETAIL`. It stays refused: the stricter reading, and not needed for
  the hospital's own patients.
