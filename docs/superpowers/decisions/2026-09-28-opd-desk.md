# /opd/desk — the queue desk, not a second registration desk (UX-AUDIT 2026-09-28)

**DECIDED 2026-09-28 — option (a).** `/opd/desk` is rebuilt as the OPD floor's QUEUE desk in the
owner's counter layout. It no longer opens visits; that door is Desk One (`/counter`).

## What was measured

| Job                                    | `/opd/desk` before | Desk One (`/counter` and its seats) |
|----------------------------------------|--------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Find the patient                       | yes                | yes (search stage)                  |
| Open a walk-in visit with a doctor     | yes                | yes (appointment stage, walk-in half, triage-ranked board, wait bars) |
| Payer / referral on the visit          | yes                | yes (walk-in body carries both; payer benefits on the dossier) |
| Check in today's appointment           | yes                | yes (`checkInAppointment`)          |
| Token slip, hand-off to billing        | yes                | yes (billing stage, payment gate)   |
| Doctors-today board with waits         | yes                | yes (routing rail)                  |
| One doctor's live queue, priority order| **yes**            | no                                  |
| Now serving, fee stamp per token       | **yes**            | no                                  |
| Class-0 escalation flash from the bay  | **yes**            | no                                  |
| Abandon a waiting token, with reason (K45) | **yes**        | only its own just-made seating      |
| E2 bulk queue transfer with consent (K44) | **yes**         | no                                  |

Everything above the line is a duplicate of Desk One, which the owner ruled is the front desk's one
screen (FD-9, 2026-09-03: *"keep the new design not the old one"*). Everything below it exists
nowhere else. The server's own figures already say what this route is for: `desk-provider.ts` links
"waiting", "sessions open" and "still here" here, and `keyboard.tsx` records `Alt+D` as "the
supervisor's OPD board".

## The standard answer

An Indian corporate hospital separates the front office (registration, appointment, billing — one
person or three chairs) from the OPD floor coordinator, who watches every consultant's line, moves a
queue when a doctor is called to the ward or is on leave (with the patients' consent), and closes a
token for a patient who has gone home. That is two screens for two jobs, so (b) — mounting Desk One
here — would give the coordinator a registration desk and delete the only queue tools there are.

## What changed

- Layout: the station shell (`components/station/station-shell.tsx`, the owner's 2026-09-25 counter
  ruling already drawn once for the lab). Header = OPD's screens (this desk, Desk One, appointments,
  display). Left lane 296px = the token in hand, or the floor's day. Centre = one numbered flow
  (1 department, 2 doctor, 3 act) with a pinned action bar. Right 352px = the chosen doctor's queue in
  the server's priority order with a source chip per row, then "Clocks running" collapsed. Below
  1280px the list is a drawer.
- Removed from this route: the patient picker, arrivals/check-in, payer/referral form, per-row "Open
  visit" and the token slip. The header link "Open a visit" goes to `/counter`, and the patient page's
  onward "Open visit" button now goes there too — same grant (`opd.visits.open`). Neither screen
  restores the patient in hand today (`/counter` deliberately does not; only the seats do), so the
  clerk searches once either way and nothing the button did before is lost.
- Kept, unchanged on the wire: K45 (no abandon request without a reason), K44 (no transfer request
  without consent; the control is shown to everyone and a 403 is read inline), the realtime + 15 s
  poll pair, the escalation flash and its per-doctor reset, the fee stamps (null renders nothing).
- Permission unchanged: `opd.visits.open`.

## Smaller calls, DECIDED

- "Clocks running" raises itself when anyone in the chosen doctor's line has waited over 60 minutes —
  the common NABH-era OPD waiting-time target in Indian corporate hospitals.
- Source chips map the server's `queueClass`: 0 DANGER (red), 1 RETURNED WITH RESULTS (green),
  2 APPOINTMENT DUE (gold), 3 WALK-IN (grey), 4 FUTURE APPOINTMENT (faint).
- The list is the chosen doctor's line, not the whole department's: one read per doctor per 15 s
  across a department would multiply the load for a view nobody acts on at once.
