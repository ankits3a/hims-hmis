# 0052 — DDInter major interactions as a second layer; the Indian drug-reference gap filled from free, legal sources in order

- **Date:** 2026-10-07   **Status:** Ruled
- **Area:** formulary, opd, doctor, pharmacy

## The owner's words (2026-10-07)

- "yes. I will go with your recommendation" — to both recommendations below.

## Decision

1. **DDInter's MAJOR interactions are used as a second layer** under the 400 curated rules, after name
   mapping. The licence is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; the owner accepts internal hospital use as non-commercial.
2. **The Indian gap is filled from free, legal sources in a fixed order**, and the owner sends two
   emails: to the Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission for permission to use the National Formulary of
   India 2026, and to CIMS India for a quote.

## Decided by delegation (how the rulings are applied)

- Major pairs only, in their own table. Curated rules always win and are never overridden without
  review. Moderate, Minor and Unknown are not loaded.
- The prescription check shows them as "Reference interaction (DDInter)" with the source; an override
  needs a reason. Attribution and the share-alike notice stay with the data.
- Before it is switched on, historical lines are replayed. If the warning rate would more than double,
  it comes back to the owner first. It has a kill switch.
- Source order for India-only medicines: combination banner with CDSCO approval status; NHS Medicines
  A–Z (OGL v3); EMA product information; Medsafe New Zealand data sheets, verbatim; LiverTox and
  LactMed; National Formulary of India 2026 only after permission; a CIMS India licence on a quote.
  Wikipedia as a labelled last resort needs the owner's separate yes.
- Not permitted: scraping Apollo, 1mg or Medindia. The BNF and Martindale need paid licences.

## Why

The supplier's file was checked on 2026-10-07: its drug text is 6 template profiles over 10,303
generics and is never used. Its interaction table is real (DDInter 2.0, 236,834 pairs, 39,482 major)
but uses US/INN names and lacks common Indian medicines. Loading only the major pairs adds real
safety cover without burying doctors in warnings.
