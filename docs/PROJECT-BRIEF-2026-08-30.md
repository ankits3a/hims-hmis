### 0.1 One-paragraph summary

HMIS is a greenfield hospital management / operating system for a new private hospital in India, replacing an unused Java-EE fork (crk-hmis) with a TypeScript **modular monolith** (NestJS + PostgreSQL 16 + Drizzle + React/Vite) whose spine is a **transactional-outbox event log** (~330-event catalogue), a **versioned workflow engine** and a **generic approvals engine**, all designed so that a tier of AI agents (T0–T4, clinical capped at T2–T3) can run the hospital's operations end-to-end on top of a deterministic substrate. The hospital is ~100 OPD/day, 10 beds and 20 users today (Aug 2026) and is planned to reach 610 beds, 2,000+ OPD/day, 300 concurrent users, 24×7 ED and 10 OTs by ~Aug 2027. The owner has no developers: every line of spec, plan, code, test and review is produced by AI agents under a documented method (EXECUTE-METHOD v3) with a token stop-loss, two independent review passes per phase, gate reports and a public lessons ledger. In 20 days the project went from a spec to a production system with 145 tables, 48 migrations, ~370 test files, 39 SPA routes and 31 seeded roles, covering registration, OPD, tariff/GST billing, memberships/accruals, notifications, user administration, resource registry, materials, mini-OT day-care, patient identity spine, kernel orders, and a full LIMS (order → accession → result → verified report) that is code-complete and awaiting a second human administrator to go live.

## 1.1 The problem being solved

**The incumbent.** The hospital runs **crk-hmis**, a fork of `hmislk/hmis` — a Java EE / JSF / PrimeFaces monolith with server-rendered UI, no India-specific features, and structural lag from per-click server round-trips. It is barely used and "holds no data worth migrating" (spec §1), so the replacement is a greenfield build with a hard cutover; a read-only crk-hmis archive is retained (spec fix D-43), and old UHIDs live on as a cross-reference field.

**Why existing Indian HMIS products were rejected (spec §2 "Rejected alternatives").**
- *Assembled open-source suites* (OpenMRS + OpenELIS + Odoo + dcm4chee, Bahmni-style) are fastest to feature parity but "repeat crk-hmis's disease" — multiple heavyweight stacks, fragile inter-system sync, and a UX that can only be skinned, never owned. Worst fit for a solo maintainer whose #1 priority is UX.
- *Microservices from day one* turn cross-module consistency into a distributed-systems problem and dump the on-prem ops burden (broker, discovery, per-service failover) on one person.
- The general grievance, restated across the specs: Indian products leak money (packages are "the #1 leakage door in Indian hospitals", §11.4 map 6), keep registers by hand, cannot be audited from the event trail, and have no notion of an agent acting inside the system.

**The hospital's profile and targets (spec §1).**
- Today: **~100 OPD visits/day, 10 beds, ~20 concurrent users; a licensed blood bank already operating; NABH accreditation underway.**
- 1-year target (~Aug 2027): **610 beds, 2,000+ OPD/day, ~300 concurrent users, 24×7 ED, 10 operation theatres, full diagnostics + pathology.**
- Real floor list reconciled 2026-08-11 (§11.19-A): reception · emergency (3 ED theatres, emergency radiology, trauma) · OPD · diagnostics & imaging · blood bank · central lab · physiotherapy · OT floor · mother & child · cancer floor with radiation oncology (LINAC + brachytherapy) · heart floor + cath lab · dialysis · endoscopy · ICU floor (3 halls × 15 beds) · private ward · general ward · pharmacy shop · waste storage · laundry · medical gas control · materials. Canteen, parking and staff housing are out of scope by decision.

**Priorities, in the owner's order (spec §1):** user experience · information sync between modules · medical-device integration · data portability · fast recovery from server failure · cheap addition of new modules.

**Constraints.** Built and maintained by the owner directing AI coding agents with no developer staff, so "architecture must favour boring, consolidated, heavily-documented technology." Hosted on-premises as the end state; "patient care must never depend on internet connectivity." GST-correct billing from day one; ABDM-ready data model from day one with actual ABDM/NHCX wiring only after external approvals land ("go-live never blocks on approval timelines").

**Who the users are.** The S10 staffing book (`2026-08-11-hmis-staffing-kpi-design.md`) defines **39 full-depth role cards** grouped as: front office & revenue (registration clerk, front-office supervisor, cashier, billing supervisor, TPA desk, admission/bed-board clerk, MRD officer) · doctors (OPD consultant, duty medical officer, ER physician, intensivist, surgeon, anaesthetist, obstetrician) · diagnostics (radiologist, pathologist, lab technician, imaging technician, blood-bank officer) · nursing (staff nurse, ICU nurse, OT nurse, ward in-charge, matron) · pharmacy & materials (pharmacist, storekeeper, purchase officer, CSSD technician) · clinical support (dietician, physiotherapist) · operations (duty manager, housekeeping, biomedical engineer, security) · governance (vitals-desk assistant, phlebotomist, quality manager/NABH, infection-control nurse, medical superintendent). Patients and attendants are users too: the communication matrix (§11.13) gives them welcome/UHID, token + live queue link, report-ready PDFs, daily running bill on WhatsApp, discharge summaries and recalls — in their registered language (Hindi/English day one) — and the patient self-service track (memory `patient-self-service-2026-08-27`) has all rulings locked. The owner receives real-time alerts (primary down, disaster declared, break-glass used) plus a **daily 8 a.m. WhatsApp digest**.

## 1.2 The vision and design philosophy

**Vision (spec §1, sharpened 2026-08-11):** "an **agentic AI operating system that runs a live hospital end-to-end** — a unified intelligence layer connecting clinical decisions, administrative workflows, and operational infrastructure." The architectural consequence is stated immediately: *agents do not replace the deterministic core; they require it.* The event log, the workflow engine and permission-enforced APIs are "the substrate the agents stand on." Priorities inside the vision: speed and accuracy delivered through auditable flow design — "no leakages, no loopholes." The owner's ambition (memory `hmis-owner-profile`): "world's first agentic AI hospital OS; maximum upfront design certainty, time no object."

**Guiding principles that recur as design law:**
- **UX is architectural, not cosmetic (spec §15).** Hard performance budgets enforced by tests: patient search < 300 ms, interactive response < 100 ms (CI-enforced from Plan 05; measured at 1,000,000 rows: phone search median 6.4 ms, `getPatient` 1.6 ms). Registration and billing counters are keyboard-first; ward tablets get large touch targets; role-based workspaces (a role logs into its queue, not a menu tree); Hindi/English switchable UI; print is a first-class surface and every printed document carries a signed QR.
- **Exception-first design.** The owner's stated team norm: "always, always consider real-world practical exceptions." §11 carries 13 locked exception maps and eight stress passes (five solo, then three adversarial swarms of 48, 67 and 50 agents that folded **125 verified fixes**; §11.20 declares convergence and closes the swarm phase).
- **Industry-standard Indian corporate hospital defaults.** Owner standing rules (memory `owner-standing-rules-planning`, 2026-08-28): when a fork is unclear, "choose the most logical option — usually the way Indian hospitals work — and record it as a planner decision"; assume the hospital holds every standard certificate and machine (MTP approved-place, PCPNDT registration, autoclave with BI reader); run an explicit edge-case pass before any final phase doc. The memory `close-open-items-completely` adds: "I cannot move to any other plan until all the grievances are solved. not partially, but completely," with a delegation to decide judgment calls and name which decisions were the owner's.
- **Leakage principle (§10.1):** every item-movement event must terminate on a patient bill or a named cost centre; leakage becomes a variance report (issued vs billed vs counted), never a mystery. Corollary (§11.11): "billing is a read model of care events" — nothing is remembered onto a bill; the **daily orphan report** lists chargeable events with no charge.
- **Structure everywhere, alerts selective (§10.3).** Every state carries an SLA and every breach is recorded; active alerting at go-live only on patient-facing waits, ER triage < 5 min, lab TAT and oxygen stock, because "alarm fatigue is the documented killer of hospital alerting."
- **Treatment first, money later** on the ER map; care never stops on deposit exhaustion; body release is never gated on payment (D-33).
- **Buy, don't build, commodity infrastructure (§9):** IP-PBX for voice (~₹1–2L), Tally for statutory accounting (HMIS exports vouchers), HR/payroll/biometric SaaS, vendor R&V/TPS for the LINAC, Orthanc + OHIF for PACS, and — after the 2026-08-20 review — **licensed clinical knowledge content** (drug interactions, SNOMED CT via NRCeS, LOINC, ICD-10) "never authored."
- **Boring, consolidated technology** for a solo maintainer; a "solo-maintainer decade" lens was one of the sixteen in stress pass 7.

## 1.3 The approved architecture

### Decision: hybrid — modular monolith core + edge services at physics boundaries (spec §2)
- The core — all administrative, clinical-record and financial workflow — is **one TypeScript application over one PostgreSQL database**. ACID transactions guarantee financial and narcotics audit integrity; modules share data without network calls.
- Edge services exist only where a protocol or data-physics boundary forces them: lab analyzers (ASTM/HL7/serial/USB on a fanless mini-PC with a SQLite buffer), ICU telemetry (Mosquitto MQTT → TimescaleDB, a separate Postgres instance), DICOM imaging (Orthanc PACS on its own volume). Each edge can crash without taking the hospital down; edges buffer locally while the core restarts.
- Standing rule: **a new module goes in the monolith by default; it becomes a service only if hardware or data physics demands it.** No message broker, no service mesh, no per-module databases.
- "One codebase, several processes" (v4.3): API · WebSocket hub · outbox/job worker · PDF renderer, all from the same build; coordination stays in Postgres. **pg-boss is dead (v4.7)** — Plan 08.5's FORK-B killed it by measurement (ESM-only, jest could not parse it); jobs run on `kernel/worker/scheduler.ts`, an advisory-lock loop where "the lock is an efficiency, not a correctness dependency."

### Tech stack (spec §5, as shipped)
| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|

| Core backend | NestJS ^11 (`apps/core`) — module system maps 1:1 to the module framework |
| Database | PostgreSQL 16; JSONB for FHIR-shaped documents; full-text search for MRD |
| DB access | Drizzle ORM (typed, SQL-first, explicit migration files — prod is measured at 48 migration files in the checkout, `0047_radiology_core.sql` latest) |

Repository layout today: `apps/core/src/kernel/{alerts, approvals, auth, db, desk, episodes, events, inference, modules, notify, ops, orders, phi, realtime, report, resources, retention, search, worker, workflow}` and `apps/core/src/modules/{billing, formulary, lab, materials, membership, opd, ot, partners, patients, pcpndt, radiology, tariff}`.

### Kernel vs modules (spec §4)
- **A module is a folder** owning its tables (no other module may touch them — lint-enforced), API routes, UI screens, event definitions and a **manifest** declaring menu entries, permissions introduced, event subscriptions and (since Plan 11h) search providers. Adding a module = add folder + migrations + register manifest; no infrastructure change.
- Modules communicate only two ways: another module's declared `index.ts` interface (e.g. `patients.get(id)`) or events.
- The **shared kernel** holds the patient master seam, event log, workflow engine, auth/RBAC, GST engine, approvals engine, notifications gateway, operating modes, the resource registry, the order envelope and the PHI audit.

### The event log — the spine (spec §4, §10.5, §10.6)
- Transactional outbox: every significant fact is appended to `events` **in the same transaction** as the state change. Consumers: modules, WebSocket pushes, WhatsApp/SMS queue, ABDM care-context notifications, analytics, and the agent layer — one mechanism supplies sync, real-time UI, engagement, audit trail and integration.
- Grammar: `entity.verb_past` (`patient.registered`, `result.verified`). Envelope: `event_id` (ULID) · `name` · `version` · `occurred_at` / `recorded_at` (distinct — downtime backfill depends on it) · `actor` (user | agent | system) · `patient_id?` · `encounter_id?` · `correlation_id` (workflow instance) · `causation_id` · `module` · typed `payload` · `site_id`. Append-only; additive fields keep v1, breaking changes mint v2.
- The reconciled catalog reached **~330 events** by spec v4.5; the shipped code counted 63 `defineEvent(` declarations at the 2026-08-20 review.
- Engineering (§11.18 sweep #10, resolved 2026-08-12): `events` is partitioned **monthly by `recorded_at`** with a dispatcher pruning floor; global idempotency lives in a side table `event_idempotency`; per-consumer delivery claims in `event_deliveries`; poison rows park in `event_dead_letters`. Cursor-based dispatcher (`event_cursors`) with a look-back window for out-of-order commits.

### Workflow engine (spec §10.2; Plan 03)
- Every flow is a **workflow definition**: states, transitions, allowed roles per transition, SLA per state, escalation ladder per SLA. Definitions are **versioned data in the DB** (draft → approve per change class → activate; immutable once active; one active version per key). In-flight instances pin their version; D-11 adds approval-gated migrate/abort.
- Humans and agents execute the same definitions through the same APIs.
- (v4.6) **"Every SLA-bearing lifecycle is a workflow instance, never a status column."** `workflow_instances` is the uniform journey-state table; modules mirror engine state (`opd_encounters.status` is a read model) and never own it. Recorded Phase-1 exception: billing's cashier session / refund voucher / recon.
- Tables: `workflow_definitions`, `workflow_instances`, `workflow_timers`, `workflow_definition_approvals`, `workflow_transitions`. SLA timers are DB rows; `runDueTimers()` emits `sla.breached` and climbs ladders; escalation resolves to the on-duty holder of a role, never a named person, with a dead-end fallback to duty manager + owner SMS (fix 11).
- Governance (§10.4, D-15): department heads and agents may *draft*; activation passes through the approvals engine. **Change classes:** Class A (clinical-safety, money rules, statutory config) needs owner + medical-superintendent two-key; Class B (operational thresholds within pre-approved bands) needs department head + duty approval; Class C (routine master data) is automated with sampled audit.

### Approvals engine (spec §8; Plan 04)
One generic mechanism: request → approver role → approve/reject with a mandatory note → event. Approvers act only inside the HMIS; WhatsApp/SMS only notifies. Every request type is registered configuration backed by an `approval_<typeKey>` workflow definition, so closure SLAs and ladders come from the engine. Anti-structuring (C-12): thresholds evaluate cumulative same-patient / same-payee / same-IST-day amounts. Urgency classes `routine | urgent | emergency` fixed per type; emergency types ride an interrupting channel with act-first-review-after. Tables `approvals`, `approval_types`; SoD pairs (`assertNotSodPair`) enforced at request time. Day-one consumers: discount overrides, refunds, patient merge/unmerge, tariff revisions, cashier variance.

### Actor fabric and RBAC (spec §14; Plan 02)
- Permissions are **action + scope** (own department / floor / hospital-wide); roles bundle permissions; users hold roles per department/floor; permission strings are declared on module manifests and synced by `syncPermissions`. Guard: `@RequirePermission("billing.refund.approve", "department")`; `CurrentActor` returns a contracts `Actor` (four members after 22c-A).
- No shared accounts, ever; PIN/badge fast user-switching (< 2 s) on shared ward terminals identifies but does not sign. TOTP second factor for signature-class and money-class permission checks (D-27).
- **Break-glass**: ER staff can open any record instantly; loudly logged and queued for review; `auth.break_glass.use` is deliberately granted to no clinical role until a route sets `breakGlassBypass` (README).
- **Agents are first-class actors**: own identities (`agents` table, `x-agent-key`), own grants (`agent_permissions`, Plan 12a), same RBAC path, per-agent kill switch, plus a global halt. A delegation seam allows an agent to act on behalf of a user with effective permissions = user ∩ agent grants and both identities stamped in audit.
- Segregation of duties: nine RBAC-enforced hard pairs (requester/approver, cashier/refund-void approver, PO approver/GRN receiver, custodian/counter, narcotics issuer/witness, payout preparer/approver, definition drafter/activator, auditor/audited, downtime declarer/downtime-cash reconciler) firing `sod.violation_blocked`.
- Tables: `users`, `roles`, `permissions`, `role_assignments`, `break_glass_grants`, `agents`, `agent_permissions`.

### Identifier grammar (README, owner rulings 2026-08-25)
- **UHID:** `<PREFIX><7-digit serial><Verhoeff check digit>` — production prefix `U`, e.g. `U12345013`; nine characters, no separators, because a UHID is typed into a search box more than read off a card. Serials 1–1,234,500 are reserved and carry no meaning (deliberately *not* a VIP or membership band — status is revocable, a UHID is not). Search accepts the bare serial, no check digit, trailing 4+ digits, stray spaces/hyphens.
- **Episode numbers:** `<letter><YYMMDD><4-digit daily serial>`, eleven characters, keyed on service date: `V` visit (`V2608250147`), `A` appointment, `L` lab order, `S` specimen/accession, `R` radiology order, `P` pharmacy dispense. One encounter = one visit number including same-day re-entry. `episode_series` is a separate table from `document_series`.
- **Invoice numbers:** `INV/2627/000123` — a gapless per-fiscal-year consecutive serial because GST demands it; "the two counters answer to different authorities."
- Every printed document carries a **HMAC-signed QR** (UHID + visit + document ID); a photographed static code fails the scan (E-23); `qr.signature_failed` is evented.

### Real-time (README "Realtime protocol")
One endpoint `/ws`; first frame must be `auth` within 5 s (token never in the URL); topic prefixes carry a required permission (`queue:<doctorId>:<date>` → `opd.queue.read`, `display:<roomId>`, `encounter:<id>`, `alerts:<userId>` per-user authorised). Pushes are hints; every screen polls its read model every 15 s. Fan-out reads the `events` table through a per-process tail, so no sticky sessions and no broker.

### Runtime loop and notifications (Plans 08.5, 10)
- Worker process `apps/core/src/worker.ts` boots a providers-only Nest context from the same build; six sweeps on advisory-lock intervals: `runDispatchCycle` 2 s · `runDueTimers` 20 s · `sweepExpiredTempRoles` 60 s · `sweepGuardianMajority` 00:05 IST · `sweepAppointmentNoShows` 23:55 IST · `runDailyClose` 23:59 IST. `scheduler_heartbeats` feeds `/health` (`not_running | ok | stale`; never `down` — the worker is never load-bearing for a human flow; when it is down, timers accumulate and drain on restart).
- First consumer: `escalation.triggered` → `alerts` rows → `alerts:<userId>` → the bell in the Shell.
- Plan 10 gateway: provider-agnostic outbound queue riding the worker; fallback ladder **WhatsApp → SMS → IVR → manual-notify desk flag**; per-patient language; quiet hours 21:00–08:00 IST for non-urgent messages; DPDP split (transactional always, promotional strictly opt-in); deceased-patient suppression. The public read-only relay (E-1) was split out and waits on the DMZ-vs-cloud decision.

### Audit, PHI and confidentiality
- **Audit is structural (§6):** event log + append-only financials + row-level `updated_by`/`updated_at` — NABH-grade traceability without a separate audit subsystem; six billing tables are immutable by trigger (migration 0012). Corrections use the **entered-in-error grammar** (E-8): flag → reversing event → linked corrected entry, never edits or deletes. Daily hash-chain anchor of the event log and a court-production workflow (D-22).
- **Confidential records (§14):** staff-as-patients and VIP records restricted beyond RBAC (`patients.is_confidential`), aliases on public surfaces, displays announce tokens only. A **sealed event class** for HIV Act / MTP / PCPNDT records (C-5), with the treating-team carve-out (E-4) and seal propagation to every derived surface (D-25).
- **PHI reads are logged** — `kernel/phi` (migration `0038`, Plan 07a) records confidential-diagnosis reads; break-glass and access-review reports (E-29) generalise it.
- **Late-entry rule:** late documentation is dual-stamped (`occurred_at` vs `recorded_at`) and flagged; backdating is structurally impossible.

### Idempotency and money integrity
- Idempotency keys on all edge-submitted events (`event_idempotency`); billing write routes accept an optional `Idempotency-Key` header (migration 0013, `idempotency_keys` table) — a reused key with a different body answers `409 idempotency_key_reused`; every web `SubmitButton` is single-flight and mints one key per attempt.
- Money is integer paise; billing is double-entry and append-only; invoices immutable, corrections via credit notes; tariff versioning with admission-date lock for IPD; best-single-benefit adjustment rules, no stacking; mixed tenders per invoice; cashier sessions with float, denomination count and variance approval.

## 3.1 The OPD front desk and doctor UX — 07a, 07b, 07c, 07d

### 3.1.1 The OPD counter UX diagnosis (2026-08-28)

**Trigger.** The owner reported that a multi-role counter clerk (registration + appointments + billing) could not move fast and that walk-in checkout was tedious for staff and patient. The build at `69dde01` was audited by nine read-only agents (1.147M tokens) and published as the artifact "The Walk-In Problem".

**Measured root cause.** Every web screen was a 1:1 projection of a backend module; there was no shared patient/encounter context anywhere in the SPA — four independent `PatientPicker` mounts, each with private `useState`. The simplest walk-in (returning patient, self-pay, cash) cost **3 route changes, 3 searches for the same person, 1 hand-typed encounter id, and 7 backend calls in 7 separate transactions**.

**Three rails built server-side and never wired** (the cheapest repair surface in the tree): `/billing?encounterId=` (documented in the router, read by the billing screen, covered by tests, constructed nowhere); `switchWithPin`/`switchWithBadge` for shared terminals (no UI); `secondFactor` on `PermissionGuard` (zero routes, deliberately — no TOTP enrolments).

**Two structural blockers.** `registered → waiting` was granted only to `vitals_desk`/`nurse`/`doctor` in the OPD workflow definition, so a front-office clerk could not finish a walk-in (dual authorisation: HTTP permission + workflow transition role). `registerPatient` had no duplicate check and no idempotency key, so a retry after a timeout silently minted a second UHID.

**Money defect found alongside the UX.** Cash overpayment was silently banked as a patient advance — the ledger held an "unallocated receipt balance" while the cashier handed the change back off-system, so drawer and ledger disagreed by exactly the change. A first note ("there is no change calculator") was later **corrected**: `issueInvoice` *does* compute `unallocatedPaise`; the real defect was *two outcomes with one record* — no change-out concept existed, and `expectedCash(float, cashTenders, cashVouchers)` had no term for it.

**Perf.** One fee quote fired ~10 sequential DB queries (reloading the entire service catalogue and every GST category, fetching the encounter twice), and `issueInvoice` re-ran the whole pricing pipeline.

**Four owner rulings (2026-08-28)** that shaped 07b: **R-1** the front desk does NOT record vitals (dedicated staff; overrode the recommendation and shrank scope — no Class A workflow change); **R-2** pay-before-consult holds — nobody passes the counter unbilled, with three lawful exits already honoured by `gate.ts` (settled · credit-extended with reason · free revisit inside the follow-up window, spec:224); **R-3** UPI collected and entered manually, `tender_ref_required` stays mandatory (dropping it would leave a hole `recon.ts` and 22a-1's settlement import inherit); **R-4** one staffer holds all three roles — roles stay granular, composed in the UI. Open item **O-1**: R-2 + R-4 mean a cashier drawer-variance lockout closes registration too; the owner must name who covers (later ruled: `billing_manager`, see 07c).

The diagnosis also produced the doctor-cockpit reality check that gated 07d: drug availability is buildable (`items.formulary_medicine_id` FK, `GET /materials/stock/balances`) but production had **zero `formulary_medicines`, zero materials items, and no human holders of `pharmacy`/`storekeeper`**; investigations could not be built because no lab/radiology module existed and `services` held 2 rows (both OPD consultation).

### 3.1.2 Plan 07a — the read gate (security hotfix)

*`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-28-phase1-07a-read-gate-hotfix.md`* — 3 tasks, all CRITICAL, migration `0038`. Written as a separate phase because "folding a confidentiality fix into a UI phase is how such fixes get deprioritised."

**The finding.** `GET /opd/patients/:id/timeline` returned diagnosis + ICD-10 for every past visit, gated only by `opd.visits.read` — a permission `front_office` holds — via `resolvePatientId` (merge mapping, documented "no gate") with its `actor` parameter threaded in and never used. The identical bug had been found and fixed in the search lane at Plan 11h close and never carried across. PHI reads were effectively unaudited (`recordOpened` fired only from the command palette); break-glass was fully built with zero routes opting in.

**What shipped** (`f17d66e`, `9e1f196`, `06586fa`, `9f86c9d`):
- **T1** — `patientTimeline` resolves through `getPatient` (existence-hides sealed); new `modules/opd/read-gate.ts` exports `encounterVisibleTo`, reused by `listVitals`/`listPrescriptions`, which now take an actor. Lesson recorded: *presence of an actor is not evidence of a gate.* Six mutants died.
- **T2** — `phi_access_log` table (migration `0038`), `kernel/phi/audit.ts` (`recordPhiAccess` never throws; `prunePhiAccessLog` is legal-hold clamped), a **care-context provider registry** in the kernel (`patients` cannot import `opd` — the cycle is real — so modules register an answer and the kernel composes), context as three values `treating|serving|none` rather than the plan's boolean (a boolean would flag every clerk read as out-of-context). **Design decisions.** DD1 gate at the read function, not the controller; DD2 existence-hiding; DD3 access is *logged, not blocked* ("blocking clinical reads is how people get hurt") — care-relationship gating on reads is a deliberate choice, not an oversight; DD4 the access log is its own stream, not `events`; DD6 no permission granted or revoked.

- **WaitAnswer — "How long will I have to wait?"** Four numbers are real (`waitingCount`, `nowServing`, `waitingVitals`, session status from `summaryByDoctor`); the fifth ("about 55 minutes") was invented. `opd_queue_entries` has carried `called_at`/`done_at` since go-live, so the estimator is one reader, no migration: trailing MEDIAN of the last 10 per doctor per SESSION, shown as a RANGE rounded up to the half hour ("an hour to an hour and a half"), suppressed under four samples, never below the position count. Lead with the position; the time is the second sentence. DECIDED: until the reader exists the console shows no duration.
- **Wayfinding — "Where is Radiology?" — routes as speech.** No floor plan exists and no printer at the desk, so a route is a sentence said while pointing, stored as two strings and a number per resource in the existing `attributes` jsonb (~40 rows). DECIDED: Emergency is not a route — she walks them.
- **PhiLine — "The line she cannot cross".** One rule: **the boundary is identity, not information.** About-the-hospital (timings, fees, bed counts, directions) goes to anyone on any channel; the moment a question names or confirms a person it becomes a disclosure needing someone she can identify. Four askers: the patient with an ID (UHID, name/age, visit today, which counter — never their diagnoses/results/bills); an attender (everything about the hospital, never whether the person is here at all; no third-party search field exists); a caller (the whole person-side of the console is sealed while the line is the active channel); police/employer/journalist (nothing; the Duty Manager). Three corrections to v1: the UHID card showed a full mobile (the shipped provider masks to four digits — "mobile in, UHID out" is a re-identification primitive); "next free about 16:00 — 2 discharges planned" is a promise the hospital did not make; a confidential patient is excluded from directory results. Needs that do not exist: an enquiry role; a separately audited directory read with its own `PhiSurface` `directory.lookup` (because `careContextFor` returns `none` for anyone with no live visit, and forty `none` rows an hour would drown the access-review worklist).
- **Grievance — the only unprompted thing the hospital hears.** Six fields, four optional, under 40 seconds; the no-printer acknowledgement is a four-digit number said twice plus an SMS. DECIDED on Indian corporate practice: routine answered in 7 working days, serious in 24 h by the Duty Manager, harm becomes an incident for the MS; unanswered on day 7 it escalates itself.
- **WhenItBreaks — the doctor walked out.** The queue rescue is the Duty Manager's (v2 page 3); this seat's job is that twelve people get the same true answer inside ten seconds, so the alert ARRIVES rather than being searched for. The second asker (walked in, not yet registered) can still be turned toward Dr Maan — no refund, no token transfer.
- **EdgeCases — thirty-four ways the seat goes wrong**, run BEFORE the console was drawn (the owner's edge-case-pass standing rule). Six changed the layout (red flags above the department; the fixed right rail; Away as a state; every live answer stamped with when it was true; the wall must work with the power off). Four marked NOT SOLVED on purpose: a language she does not speak, eight people at once with no token system, NEET-season volume, a deaf visitor. **The one operational hole and its ruling: the IPD counter shuts at 20:00 and this seat runs to 08:00, exactly when people arrive off a late bus having been telephoned. Options were weighed; option 1 CHOSEN — "the ward desk owns it after 20:00, formally — free, and the ward carries it". RULED: the ward desk absorbs it after 20:00, so the enquiry seat never sends anybody to a dark counter; it sends them UP, with the ward and the sister's name.** Noted as assumed-not-agreed: the ward sister has not signed it.

### 5.2.4 How a module is structured (conventions)

- **`manifest.ts`** — exports `<name>Manifest: ModuleManifest`:
  ```ts
  type ModuleManifest = {
    key: string; title: string;
    menu: { label: string; path: string; permission: string }[];   // SPA paths + guarding permission
    permissions: string[];                                          // the ONLY source of permission strings
    subscriptions: { event: string; consumer: string }[];           // dispatcher consumers
    search?: SearchProvider[]; desk?: DeskProvider[];
    resourceKinds?: readonly ResourceKindDecl[]; orderKinds?: readonly OrderKindDecl[];
  };
  ```
  `syncPermissions` upserts every declared permission into the `permissions` table at seed; `grantPermissionToRole` refuses a string no manifest declares.
- **`index.ts`** — the module's ONLY public surface. ESLint `no-restricted-imports` in `apps/core/src/modules/**` forbids importing another module's internals (`**/modules/<x>/**` except `**/modules/<x>/index`). Other modules import `from "../billing"` (index) or consume events.
- **`<name>.module.ts`** — Nest `@Module` listing controllers; modules with realtime register topic spaces in an `OnModuleInit` registrar (e.g. `LabRealtimeRegistrar`).
- **Controllers** — thin: `@Controller("<prefix>")`, constructor injects `@Inject(DB) db: Db` and often `@Inject(MODULE_REGISTRY)`; each handler has `@RequirePermission("<module>.<entity>.<verb>", "hospital")`, takes `@CurrentActor() actor`, `@Body() body: unknown`, parses with a zod schema via `parsed(schema, body)` (400 on failure), wraps domain work in `withIdempotency(...)` and/or `withTx(...)`, and converts domain errors with `toHttp(e)`.
- **Domain files** (`desk.ts`, `verify.ts`, `ledger.ts` …) — pure functions `(db|tx, actor, input) → result`; they append events with `appendEvent(tx, <eventDef>.make({...}))`.
- **`errors.ts`** — a `class <Name>Error extends Error { code, detail? }` with a closed `*_ERROR_CODES` tuple and a `<name>HttpStatus(code)` map (e.g. lab codes: `permission_denied`, `unknown_orderable`, `consent_required`, `duplicate_unacknowledged`, `tube_mismatch`, `identity_recheck_required`, `absurd_value`, `sod_violation`, `already_verified`, `user_actor_required`, `report_print_blocked`, `report_not_publishable`, `release_approval_invalid` …).
- **`events.ts`** — `defineEvent("<entity>.<verb_past>", MODULE, zodPayload)`; 207 `defineEvent` sites across the tree.
- **`approval-types.ts`** — registers approval types the module needs (`registerXApprovalTypes`).
- **`workflow-def.ts`** — JSON workflow definitions (states/transitions/SLA) for the module's state machines.
- **`kinds.ts`** — `ResourceKindDecl`/`OrderKindDecl` declarations.
- **`desk-provider.ts` / `search-provider.ts`** — cards for `/me/desk` and providers for `/search`.
- **`config.ts`** — module configuration rows that the deploy's configuration gate requires.
- **Tests** live beside the code: `<file>.test.ts` (unit, jest), `*.concurrency.test.ts`/`*.contention.test.ts` (parallel-transaction races), `golden/` fixtures with `golden-*.test.ts`, and `apps/core/test/<module>.e2e.test.ts` (supertest through the real Nest app) plus `*-lifecycle.e2e.test.ts`, `*-parity.test.ts` (Caddyfile/nav/deploy parity), `perf-*.test.ts` (budgets).

### 5.2.5 The error envelope and HTTP mapping

Every module's `*-http.ts` (or controller) has:
```ts
function httpError(statusCode, message, code, detail?) → new HttpException({ statusCode, message, code, detail? }, statusCode)
function toHttp(e): never  // LabError→labHttpStatus, OrderError→orderHttpStatus, BillingError→billingHttpStatus,
                           // TariffError→tariffHttpStatus, ApprovalError→409, ResourceError→resourceHttpStatus,
                           // WorkflowError→403 if role_denied else 409; anything else rethrown (500)
```
So the wire error body is always `{ statusCode, message, code, detail? }`; the SPA reads `ApiError.body.code` and maps it to an i18n string (`labErrorText(e)` etc.). Auth failures are Nest's `401 UnauthorizedException` / `403 ForbiddenException`.

### 5.2.6 `packages/contracts` (shared by core and web)

- **`envelope.ts`** — `Actor = { type: "user"|"agent"|"system"|"patient"; id }` (a patient actor's `id` is the phone credential, never a patient id; the subject patient is `patientId`). `EventInput { name, version, occurredAt, actor, patientId?, encounterId?, correlationId?, causationId?, module, payload, siteId, idempotencyKey? }`. `defineEvent(name, module, payloadSchema, version=1)` validates `name` against `/^[a-z][a-z0-9_]*(\.[a-z][a-z0-9_]*)+$/` ("lowercase `entity.verb_past`") and returns `{ name, module, version, payloadSchema, make(args) }`; `make` zod-parses the payload and defaults `siteId: "main"`.
- **`ids.ts`** — `newEventId()` and `newId()` both return a ULID. ULIDs are NOT monotonic; never `ORDER BY id` for recency — tables carry a `bigserial seq`.
- **`registry.ts`** — `EventRegistry { register(def), get(name), names() }` (duplicate registration throws).
- **`search.ts`** — `SearchEntity` union (`patient|doctor|department|appointment|invoice|service|approval|user|room|command|instrument`), `SearchChip`, `SearchQuery {raw, text, chips, range?, entities?, limit}`, `SearchHit {entity, id, title, subtitle?, meta?, href?, restricted?}`, `SearchGroup {entity, provider, hits, total, timedOut, errored}`, `SearchResponse {groups, tookMs, skipped}`, and the query grammar parser (`@doctor:<id>` chips, bare `@dept` narrowing, `today|yesterday|this week|last week` + Hindi date words). Both the palette and the server parse the same string.

### 5.2.8 Idempotency pattern

Two layers:
1. **Request-level** — `withIdempotency(db, { actorId, route, key }, body, work)` (`modules/billing/idempotency.ts`, table `idempotency_keys`, migration 0013). Claims `(actor_id, route, key)` with `onConflictDoNothing`; on conflict replays the stored response if the canonical SHA-256 `requestHash` matches, else refuses; on failure the claim is deleted so a retry can run. Controllers read the key from `@Headers("idempotency-key")` and name the route through a constant map (e.g. `LAB_IDEMPOTENT_ROUTES.deskOrder`). The web's `SubmitButton` mints a key per submit attempt via `newIdempotencyKey()` (UUID) and `api()` sends it as `Idempotency-Key`.
2. **Event-level** — `appendEvent` with `idempotencyKey` claims `event_idempotency(idempotency_key)` and returns the existing `{eventId, seq}` on a duplicate.

### 5.2.9 Audit and event naming

- The `events` table is the audit log: columns `seq bigserial`, `event_id`, `name`, `version`, `occurred_at`, `recorded_at`, `actor_type`, `actor_id`, `patient_id`, `encounter_id`, `correlation_id`, `causation_id`, `module`, `payload jsonb`, `site_id`, `idempotency_key`; PK `(seq, recorded_at)` (monthly partitions created by the worker). ### 5.4.4 Operating modes (`kernel/ops`)

`OPERATING_MODES = ["commissioning", "ramp", "normal", "degraded", "downtime"]`. Production has never left `commissioning`. The downtime protocol issues numbered paper kits (`downtime_kits`, `downtime_kit_ranges`, `downtime_form_counters`) whose serials are back-filled later; `interfaces` carry heartbeats swept by the worker.

## 5.5 How to add a new module end-to-end (the observed recipe)

1. **Schema** — add `apps/core/src/kernel/db/schema/<module>.ts` with `pgTable` definitions (ULID `text` ids, `bigserial seq` where order matters, `timestamptz`, paise integers, `site_id` default `main`), export it from `schema/index.ts`, write `schema/<module>.test.ts` (shape/constraint assertions). Run `pnpm --filter @hmis/core db:generate`, rename the SQL to `NNNN_<module>_core.sql` if you like, commit the SQL and the `_journal.json` entry together, then `db:migrate`.
2. **Module folder** `src/modules/<module>/` with: `manifest.ts` (key, title, `menu`, `permissions`, `subscriptions`, optional `search`, `desk`, `resourceKinds`, `orderKinds`), `errors.ts` (`<X>Error` + closed code tuple + `<x>HttpStatus`), `events.ts` (`defineEvent("<entity>.<verb_past>", "<module>", zod)`), domain files, `approval-types.ts` / `workflow-def.ts` if state machines or approvals are needed, `<module>-http.ts` (`parsed`, `toHttp`, `<X>_IDEMPOTENT_ROUTES`), controllers, `<module>.module.ts`, and `index.ts` exporting only the public surface. Register the manifest in `kernel/modules/manifests.ts` (`ALL_MANIFESTS`) and the Nest module in `app.module.ts`. Never import another module's internals — go through its `index.ts` or subscribe to its events.
3. **Controller rules** — `@Controller("<module>")`; every handler `@RequirePermission`; parse bodies with zod; wrap writes in `withIdempotency` (header `Idempotency-Key`) and `withTx`; append events inside the same transaction; convert errors with `toHttp`. Reads of PHI call `recordPhiAccess` with a `PhiSurface` (extend the union in `kernel/phi/audit.ts`).
4. **Numbers** — if the module issues a document number, add a key to `EPISODE_SERIES` (single letter for clinical documents) rather than a new counter; statutory numbers go through `document_series`.
5. **Orders / resources** — declare `orderKinds` (`kind`, `seriesKey`, `placePermission`) and `resourceKinds` (`statuses`, `initial`, `occupied`, `onRelease`) on the manifest; boot validation enforces uniqueness and declared permissions.
6. **Dashboard hooks** — implement `DeskProvider` (cards with `band: "now"|"today"`, rows/stats/topics) for `/me/desk`, and a `SearchProvider` (`key: "<module>.<entity>"`, `entity` from the contracts union — extend the union in `packages/contracts/src/search.ts` if new, `permission`, `run(ctx)`), both attached on the manifest. Realtime: define topic spaces + a router in `realtime.ts` and register them in an `OnModuleInit` registrar.
7. **Worker** — add sweeps to `kernel/worker/jobs.ts` (`every(ms)` or `dailyIst("HH:MM")`) and dispatcher consumers via manifest `subscriptions` (`{ event, consumer }`); consumers get `DispatchedEvent` and must be idempotent.
8. **Seeds and gates** — `scripts/seed-<module>.ts` for masters, a `config.ts` row the deploy's `check:config-present` gate requires, and role grants in `scripts/seed-roles.ts` (a permission with no role should be listed as `NotYetModelled` with a reason).
9. **Tests** — unit tests beside each file, `*.concurrency.test.ts` for any counter or state transition, `test/<module>.e2e.test.ts` with supertest, fixtures in `test/helpers/<module>.ts`; keep `pnpm verify` green (typecheck + lint + jest + vitest) against the compose DB.
10. **Web** — `lib/<module>-api.ts` (Wire types + functions through `api()`), `screens/<module>-<screen>.tsx` (+ `.test.tsx` with `renderWithProviders`/`stubFetch`), a route in `router.tsx` whose `(to, permission)` matches the manifest `menu` entry exactly, i18n keys in BOTH `en.json` and `hi.json`, a `.print-doc` component if it prints, state colours only from the four `--state-*` tokens, `SubmitButton` for every mutation. Never call `fetch` outside `lib/api.ts`; never put a screen route under `/api`.
11. **Deploy** — migrations ship inside the image; `deploy.sh` re-runs seeding and the configuration gate, then proves `/api/health` and one screen path through Caddy. Measure the production migration count; never assume it.

## Part B — The method: how one owner builds this with AI agents

### B.1 The shape of the practice

The hospital owner builds the platform solo by directing Claude Code sessions on the build host (`root@<build-host>`, checkout `/opt/hmis`, production stack `/opt/hmis-prod` on the same box, live at hmis.crkmch.com). The method has gone through three versions in nineteen days and is itself the subject of two measured reviews, a 423 KB lessons ledger, a token-audit script with a baselines file, and a set of memory files that tell the next session what not to re-learn. The output side, as METHOD-REVIEW-2 states first: "the method ships" — 13 days, 323 commits, ~32,850 lines of production TypeScript and ~34,170 lines of tests, system live in production (measured 2026-08-23; far more since).

### B.2 EXECUTE-METHOD v2 — the HEAVY-lane pipeline (2026-08-19)

Adopted after the first method review measured cost per agent tripling from 75k (Plan 04) to 204k (Plan 08) with no rise in task complexity. Five phases: **SPIKE → PLAN → COMPILE → RUN → VERIFY**. The spike (new in v2, ~50k, one agent on a throwaway branch) builds the riskiest 10% for real before the plan exists — Plan 08's false sentence about Postgres `TRUNCATE` ordering cost 934k tokens and delivered nothing; a spike would have found it for 50k. The plan carries a **risk tier per task**: CRITICAL (money arithmetic, concurrency/locking, immutability, approvals/permissions — gets mutants, measured races, fail-first, an opus coder, an opus gate) or ROUTINE (tests that pass and a mechanical check; no mutants). Per-task test-count targets were removed. COMPILE builds a Workflow-tool script whose briefs **point** at `AGENT-RULES.md` and the committed plan rather than restating them; model routing is by kind of judgement ("opus wherever correctness rests on proving an assertion has teeth; sonnet for everything else"); agents get a restricted tool set (Plan 08 carried ~11k tokens of MCP roster into every agent and made zero MCP calls). A nine-item **compile-time sweep** runs before any brief is written: resolve every path against the tree; look for forward references; grep for the losing fork branch; ask what the plan asserts about anything the spike proved unused; count tasks with no in-pipeline verdict; confirm a commit message per task; stat the template before grepping it; assert the script's `files` arrays equal the plan's File Structure both directions; check CI per commit and read more than `conclusion` (three states: green, red, did-not-run). Pre-flight (`<name>.preflight.js`) proves the script with a module-parse probe, negative controls injected and observed to fail, and a dry run asserting every rules block in the rendered brief. RUN keeps the ladder (a rung advances only on a real gate rejection; infra failures retry the same rung) and splits review into a mechanical check (main session, every task, ~5k), a per-task opus gate (CRITICAL only, ~130k), and one opus discovery review per pipeline (~150k) — because the per-task gate had not rejected in 22 consecutive tasks while costing a third of every pipeline, and had quietly become a discoverer. VERIFY is the main session's own detached `pnpm verify` with the exit value read from a file, `git show --stat` against Files lists, frozen-path audit, CI green by SHA, clean tree. Expected effect ~2.45M → ~1.3–1.5M per six-task pipeline, corrected in §8 to ~25–35% for a money module where verification depth is earned. Compiled pipelines live in `pipelines/` (plan-08 B/C, 08.5, 09, 10, 11a, each with its preflight).

### B.3 EXECUTE-METHOD v3 — the shell shrinks to fit the phase (2026-08-23)

The second review classified all 125 ledger entries and found **55% process-induced against 26% product bugs caught**, the largest class being hand-maintained copies of facts drifting across ~12 documents per phase (13 entries), then brief-compilation defects (11), gate/ladder ceremony (15), subagent coordination (6, incl. §2.40). Plan 11d's four-line behavioural delta was carried by twelve documents, `seed:roles` repeated 18 times, planning docs 1.4× the byte size of the code, a ≤3.4M budget larger than 11c's entire actual. Predictive budgets missed by up to 2.2×. v2's ratchet re-accreted within days; in the project's whole history exactly one rule had ever been removed. The owner's standing rule was that the method changes "only if the evidence is abundant" — the review is that evidence, and v3 (`c889870`) was ruled the same day.

**Principle:** move every claim from prose to execution, and keep every fact in exactly one place. Five changes:

1. **One document per phase.** Why → spike (questions before, measured answers appended in place) → design decisions → tasks (tier, Files list, acceptance criteria, commit message; CRITICAL tasks carry their Assertion Book rows inline: assertion · mutant · discriminating input) → **CLOSE** (findings as they arrive, mechanical verification, the actuals row, ledger-bound lessons — this *is* the findings inbox and the gate report). Retired: brainstorm prompt, spike brief, spike report, execute prompt, findings inbox, gate report. **The fact rule:** a SHA, a role key, a budget appears once; a second full statement is a defect.
2. **Phase tiers.** **LIGHT** (default — the main session codes sequentially, in-session, ≤8 tasks as a guide) or **HEAVY** (the v2 pipeline by pointer). The lane sets who codes; it never sets verification depth — money, locking, permissions and immutability seams get executed mutants in either lane. The LIGHT lane: session codes under AGENT-RULES in full; mutants for every inline CRITICAL row; CI watched not assumed (`ci-watch.sh`/`ci-watch-host.sh`); **one independent fresh-context reviewer after the final task** (restricted tools, no MCP roster) whose CRITICAL findings block close; mechanical close by the session; deploys owner-authorised in as many words.
3. **Checks are scripts or they are not method** — a recurring check enters only as an executable beside `ci-watch.sh`; prose checklists stop growing.
4. **The ledger stops ratcheting** — an ARCHIVE pass at every phase close strikes entries whose enabling mechanism no longer exists (struck in place, rule-6 pattern, because earlier briefs cite by number).
5. **Actuals and a stop-loss, not predictive budgets** — the actuals table continues in CLOSE; one stop-loss per phase set from the last comparable phase's per-task rate, a tripwire that halts for an owner decision.

**The topology ruling (v3 §8):** a third of AGENT-RULES, the SSH tax (65% of coder shell calls, ~42 navigation round-trips per agent) and the §2.40 phantom-mirror class existed only because authoring happened on Windows while evidence lived on the host. The owner moved authoring **onto the build host** on 2026-08-23 (Claude Code installed at `/usr/local/bin/claude`, tmux for persistence); rules 1–3 collapse to one sentence, rule 13 and all of 22(a)–(g) are struck, the CRLF class dies. The Windows checkout became read-only. A vigilance note: production paths are now reachable by native file tools, so rule 3/7 discipline carries the weight the SSH boundary used to make conspicuous.

**The pilot's refutation conditions (v3 §7),** recorded in advance: if the reviewer or production surfaces a defect class v2's per-task apparatus had a named prior catch for, the next phase re-tiers HEAVY; if a LIGHT phase's total lands inside or above the v2 band (2.64M / 2.49M / 3.34M) for comparable scope, the cost claim is refuted; transcription-class incidents target zero.

### B.4 The stop-loss formula, as amended five times

The formula is the most-amended sentence in the method, and each amendment is a measured ledger entry:

- **Original (v3 §6):** `1.5 × (last comparable phase's per-task rate × this phase's task count)`.
- **§2.95 (Plan 09, 2026-08-26):** normalise the comparable per task, then scale — Plan 09 took 1.5× a five-task phase's *total* and applied it to eight tasks; it fired mid-phase while the measured per-task rate agreed with the comparable to 1.2%. "A caveat is not a calculation."
- **§2.107 (Plan 09a):** add a **review term** — one full reviewer pass per remediation cycle, from actuals (16a: 181k one pass; 09a: 475k two passes). And the rule the number protects: a reviewer's fix is unreviewed code on the same path — send the reviewer back.
- **§2.141 (Plan 17, 2026-08-29):** add the **main-session term** — every row in `token-baselines.json` was subagent tokens, which in a LIGHT phase means the reviewer, so `per-task rate × task count` multiplied a review rate by a task count and the coding session appeared in neither term. Plan 17 spent ~482,000 on two ROUTINE tasks against a per-task budget of 30,267 — 8× over. Provisional main-session term ≈200,000 × task count. Every session now records its own token balance at kickoff and each task boundary (runbook O3's "unmeasurable from inside" is retired). **Lane consequence:** at ~200k/task a nine-task module build is two LIGHT phases; cut at a seam the contract already freezes — which is exactly how 17 became 17a (order→accession) and 17b (result→report).
- **§2.143 (Plan 17a, 2026-08-30):** the total landed at 92% of a ceiling whose terms were 27% under, 100% under (90,801 carried for subagents a LIGHT lane forbids) and 72% over — "three errors cancelling is not a validated formula." Delete the task-subagent term in a LIGHT lane; make the review term a multiplier because **nothing budgets the repair** of what the reviewer finds (twelve findings across two passes ≈ the review's own weight again). Report a fraction of stop-loss only at CLOSE.
- **§2.145 (Plan 17b, 2026-08-30):** the remediation factor is **2.0, not 1.0**, because roughly a third of pass-1 fixes are wrong (17a: 3 of 5; 17b: 4 of 13) and the fixing commit reliably makes one or two new defects; 17b's review lane cost ~965,000 against ~645,000 for all four tasks' coding.

The formula as it stands: `stop-loss = main-session term (~200k × tasks, revise every close) + 0 for task subagents in a LIGHT lane + review term × (1 + 2.0)`. A resumed reviewer is priced at ~1.3× its previous invocation (§9.5); a second *fresh* reviewer at roughly the first (§9.10).

### B.5 Review passes, reviewer briefing, and what the reviewer buys

The close review is, on the project's own data, the best value per token in any phase, and the ledger says so with receipts: 16a's single 181,605-token reviewer found **three CRITICAL patient-safety defects** in a tree with eleven green verifies, eight green CI runs and fourteen dead mutants (§2.102 — "a dead mutant confirms an assertion discriminates; it cannot tell you the assertion is aimed at the wrong thing"); 14's pass 1 returned 1 CRITICAL (a silent stock loss — two concurrent receipts into a new (store,batch) pair both read 0) + 9 MAJOR against a nine-for-nine green tree; 15's found the §269ST cash ceiling summed only from deposits *held* (22 mutants, 21 dead, and the row asked whether the refusal fires, not what the compared number was summed from — §2.128); 17a's two passes found 2 CRITICAL + 12 MAJOR with 32 dead mutants behind them; 22c-A's second fresh pass found `sql\`now()\`` is `transaction_timestamp()` so a clock-skew fix had not fixed anything.

Standing rules that grew out of this: **resume for MEMORY, spawn FRESH for SCOPE** (§2.115 — Plan 13 resumed one reviewer twice; per-call cost went 4,611 → 41,073 → 112,041 as workload fell two orders of magnitude, 604,655 total = 92% of the phase's stop-loss; a fresh agent opens at ~7k of context); **brief the second reviewer at the FIXES**, with a per-fix verdict CORRECT/INCOMPLETE/WRONG (§2.136); **brief the reviewer at the operands, not the branches** — for every threshold on a money or safety path, name what the compared quantity is summed from and one real transaction that sum omits; money file first; name the frozen interfaces consumed (§9.7); tell reviewers whether another lane is running tests and forbid them to run any; **a close-review fix is not done when it compiles** — run the suite and read the failure count (§9.8, §2.125–127); when a fix removes a disclosure, enumerate every other field that is a function of it and every caller parameter that interacts with the filter — "a filter applied after a limit is a counter" (§2.140); for every parameter a fix sets, grep the callee for every use; fix a defect class across the module in the same commit; when a fix serialises a race, name the twin and fix the frequent one first (§2.145). And **a LIGHT phase may not be called cheap and may not record an actuals row until its reviewer has run** (§9.4).

### B.9 Parallel lanes A/B and the shared checkout
