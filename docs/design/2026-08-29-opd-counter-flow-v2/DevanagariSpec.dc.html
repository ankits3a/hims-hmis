Hindi on paper and on the wall

कागज़ और पटल पर हिंदी

Setting Devanagari is not translating the strings. Eight things break when Hindi is added to a slip or a board, and seven of them are invisible on a designer's monitor and obvious on an 80 mm thermal printer. This sheet is what the token slips and the board on the previous page are built on.

1
Devanagari needs 1.5 leading. Latin's 1.3 clips it.
Matras sit above the shirorekha (ी े ै ो ौ ं ँ) and below the baseline (ु ू ृ). A line box tuned for Latin ascenders and descenders has nowhere to put them.
CLIPSline-height: 1.15
कृपया प्रतीक्षा करें
डॉक्टर शीघ्र बुलाएँगे
The ॉ of डॉक्टर and the ँ of बुलाएँगे are shaved off. On screen it looks like tight, confident typography. On paper it looks like a printer fault.
CORRECTline-height: 1.55
कृपया प्रतीक्षा करें
डॉक्टर शीघ्र बुलाएँगे
One CSS class, applied to every Devanagari run in the app. It costs about 18% more vertical space on a slip — budget for it, don't discover it.
2
At the same pixel size, Hindi reads smaller than English
Devanagari's counters are tighter and the shirorekha eats the top. Match them optically, not numerically — Hindi wants about +1 px at slip sizes, +2 at board sizes.

3
Numbers stay Western. Always. Even in the Hindi slip.
Token numbers, UHIDs, amounts, times, phone numbers. This is the single decision most likely to be got wrong out of a wish to be thorough.
NEVERदेवनागरी अंक
५३ कक्ष १०४
₹३००.००
The board shows 53, the slip shows ५३, and the patient is now holding two different numbers. Staff read the Western form aloud anyway. Barcode payloads and SMS are Western. The only thing Devanagari digits achieve is a mismatch.
ALWAYSपश्चिमी अंक
53 कक्ष 104
₹300.00
Hindi words, Western digits, in the same sentence. Wrap every numeric run in its own span so the Devanagari face never gets asked to render a digit — some Devanagari faces have poor Latin numerals and the run will change colour on you.
4
Never machine-transliterate a person's name
A wrong name on a prescription is a patient-safety event, not a typo. Capture both forms at registration or print only what was typed.

5
Your thermal printers cannot type Hindi — they can only draw it
This is a procurement fact, not a styling one, and it is worth knowing before the next printer order.
What goes wrong
An 80 mm ESC/POS printer in text mode has code pages, not Unicode — CP437, CP1252, sometimes CP1251. Send it Devanagari and it prints boxes, question marks, or Cyrillic. Nothing errors; the slip just comes out wrong, and it comes out wrong at 11 a.m. on a Monday.
What to do instead
Render the whole slip to a 1-bit raster image and send it in graphics mode (GS v 0). Shaping, matras and conjuncts are then the browser's problem, not the printer's — and the slip looks identical on every printer you ever buy.

203 dpi
Printable width
576 dots

11 pt
Conjuncts are the reason for the 11 pt floor. At 203 dpi, क्ष त्र ज्ञ द्व ट्ट श्र below 11 pt smear into a single grey mark. प्राथमिक becomes unreadable before जाँच does — so test with the conjunct-heavy words, not the easy ones.
6
Hindi labels run 30–40% wider. Fixed label columns will break.
Every counter screen in the app currently sizes label columns in fixed pixels. Those are all latent bugs the day Hindi is switched on.

It cuts both ways, which is why a blanket "add 40%" fails too. Size label columns with min-content or a grid track, never a pixel count — then Hindi is a content change instead of a layout change.
7
Hindi leads on anything a patient holds. English leads on anything staff use.
Not a toggle. A rule per surface, decided once, so no screen has to ask.
Hindi first, English gloss beneath
· Token slip and payment receipt
· Waiting-hall display board
· SMS, appointment reminders, fasting instructions
· Consent forms, patient rights, the grievance notice
· The patient-facing web page behind the QR
English only
· Every counter and clinical screen
· The prescription's clinical body and drug names
· Lab reports, units, reference intervals
· Audit trails, exports, anything a regulator reads
Drug names are never translated. पैरासिटामोल on a dispensing label is how the wrong strip gets handed over.
THE EIGHT RULES संक्षेप में
1 · One .hi class, line-height: 1.55, on every Devanagari run.
5 · Render thermal slips as a raster image, not as text.
2 · Hindi gets +1 px at slip size, +2 px on the board.
6 · Nothing Hindi below 11 pt on a 203 dpi head.
3 · Western digits everywhere, in their own span.
7 · Label columns sized by content, never by pixels.
4 · Patient names: captured, or Latin only. Never guessed.
8 · Hindi leads on paper; English leads on staff screens.
