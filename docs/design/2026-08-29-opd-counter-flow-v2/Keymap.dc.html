The counter is a keyboard, not a mouse

A counter clerk types faster than they point, does the same nine things four hundred times a day, and never looks at the screen while a patient is talking. Every action below has a key. The mouse still works — it is just no longer the fast path.

Teach it in one sitting, not in a manual
The keys print on a card that sits under the counter glass, F1 shows the same card on screen, and for the first two weeks every button on every counter screen carries its key in small grey type beside the label. Then the labels fade out. Nobody is asked to memorise anything.
