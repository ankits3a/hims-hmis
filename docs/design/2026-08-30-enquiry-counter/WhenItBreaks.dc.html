“Dr Rao has been called to Emergency — there's no fixed time for his return. Dr Maan is in Room 106 and your token will carry across. Or you can wait, but I can't tell you how long.”
WHY The reason first, the option second, the honest uncertainty third — in that order. People accept “I don't know” when it arrives after a reason and next to a choice. They do not accept it on its own, and “he'll be back soon” is the sentence that becomes complaint 4471.

“Dr Rao is in Emergency. Before you pay for a slip — Dr Maan is in the same department today, Room 106, and there are only three people there.”
Caught here, this person never registers against a doctor who is not in the building — no refund, no token to transfer, no argument at Billing. This is the highest-value sentence at the enquiry counter, and it exists only because the alert reached her before the patient did.

“Ring before you set out” is the only honest thing to say to somebody an hour away, and it costs the hotline one more call to save a wasted journey and a complaint.

Four rules for a handoff
01
A named person, never a counter number
“Ask Bhuvan at Billing” is followed. “Go to Billing” is where the bounce starts.
02
If she cannot name one, she keeps it
A held question with an owner and a time beats a confident direction to the wrong desk.
03
The second bounce is a complaint, automatically
A held question reopened twice files itself. Nobody has to be brave enough to complain about being sent in a circle.
04
Held questions are read at the end of the month
They are a list of what the lobby cannot answer — which is the cheapest signage brief and training plan this hospital will ever get.
A held question is the third write this seat needs, and it was not on anybody's list. It is also the only one that gives the hospital something back.
