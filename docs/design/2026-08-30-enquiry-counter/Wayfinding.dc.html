“Where is Radiology?”

एक्स-रे कहाँ है?

There is no floor plan anywhere in this hospital's software, and with no printer at this desk there is nothing to draw one on. So a route is not a map — it is a sentence she says while pointing, and it has to survive being repeated by a person who was half-listening, is anxious, and cannot read the signs on the way. Which makes this the cheapest thing on the whole canvas to build and the easiest to build badly.

1
The registry knows where things are. It does not know how to get there.
resources holds a floor → ward → room tree with attributes.floor, so an address is derivable. An address is not what was asked for.
DERIVED FROM THE TREE
Radiology · Block B · 2nd floor · Room 214
रेडियोलॉजी · ब्लॉक बी · दूसरी मंज़िल
Correct, and useless. “Block B” is a letter on an English sign that a first-time visitor has never seen and cannot find. The lift she needs is not in this sentence. Neither is the fact that the second-floor corridor is reached through the first-floor waiting hall, which is the part everybody gets wrong.
WRITTEN BY SOMEBODY WHO WALKED IT
मेरे पीछे वाले गलियारे से सीधे जाइए, दवा की दुकान के आगे लिफ़्ट है। दूसरी मंज़िल पर उतरकर बाएँ — नीले दरवाज़े।
“Straight down the corridor behind me, the lift is past the medicine shop. Second floor, turn left when you get out — the blue doors.”
Four landmarks, no letters, no room number until the end. She can point at the first one from her chair.
2
Five rules, and they all come from her having no paper
Every one of these is a consequence of the answer being spoken once and then walked from memory.
01
Every route starts at this counter, not at the gate.
One origin means one string per destination. It also means the first clause can be a direction she physically points in.
02
Landmarks people already name, not signage.
The medicine shop, the tea stall, the big staircase, the Hanuman shrine at the gate. Signage is in English and half of it is missing.
03
Three turns maximum, then a person to ask.
Nobody retains four. Anything further ends “…and ask at the desk there” — a handoff to a human is a legitimate last step.
04
Room number last, never first.
It is the confirmation that they arrived, not the instruction that gets them there. Leading with it is how people end up on the wrong floor reading door numbers.
05
Stairs and lift are different routes, and the screen holds both.
An 80-year-old with a stick and a young man in a hurry are not given the same sentence, and she should not have to compose the second one herself.
06
Distance in minutes of walking, not metres.
“Two minutes” sets an expectation that stops somebody turning back after thirty seconds thinking they have gone wrong.

WHAT IT NEEDS the smallest new thing on this canvas, and the only one nobody has to design a table for
Two strings and a number, on the resource row, in attributes — which is already a jsonb column with no schema to change:
routeHithe spoken route in Devanagari, from this counter
routeEnthe same, so she can check what she is saying
routeStairsnull on the ground floor; the step count where it is not
walkMinutesone integer, rounded up
About forty rows — every department, every counter, the lab, radiology, the wards, the pharmacy, the billing office, the toilets and the canteen. Written once, by somebody who walks each one, and edited from the same admin screen that already edits rooms.
The failure this prevents
Without it every one of these sentences is composed fresh, by whoever is on the desk, from an address. Two people give two different routes to the same room; the wrong one becomes the story the patient tells their family; and the day Ekta is on leave the answers get measurably worse with nobody able to say why.
The building does not change. The sentence should not either.
