The line she cannot cross

This seat can read more of the hospital than any other and is the only one with no clinical reason to read any of it. The current console states the rule in a blue band and then leaves the enforcement to Ekta's judgement forty times an hour. The screen should make the wrong thing hard, so this sheet is one rule, four askers, and the two traps that get hospitals sued.

The rule, and everything below follows from it
The boundary is identity, not information.
Doctor timings, department hours, bed counts by ward, fees, directions, which counter to go to — none of that belongs to anybody, and she gives it to anyone who asks, in any channel, all day. The moment a question names or confirms a person, it stops being an enquiry and becomes a disclosure, and a disclosure needs somebody she can identify on the other side of it.
ABOUT THE HOSPITAL
“Is Dr Rao in today?” · “How much is a consultation?” · “Are there beds in the male ward?” · “When does the lab open?” · “Where is X-ray?”
Answered to anyone, on any channel.
ABOUT A PERSON
“What is my UHID?” · “Is my brother admitted?” · “Which ward is she in?” · “Did his report come?” · “Has she been here before?”
Counter only, with a face and a card. Never on the phone.

2
“Is my brother admitted here?”
The kindest question on this list and the one that breaks the rule hardest, because the answer — either answer — is the disclosure.
WHY “NO” IS ALSO A LEAK
If she says “he isn't here” when he isn't, and stays silent when he is, then silence means yes. A rule that only holds for one of the two answers is not a rule — it is a tell, and the third person who asks will read it.
The same shape governs the confidential flag, which is why it is not a separate case: the search provider excludes a confidential record in the WHERE clause, so from this desk that patient does not exist. Ekta must not be able to tell “no such person” from “a person I am not allowed to see” — and the screen must not tell her either. Identical wording, identical timing, no extra spinner.
WHAT SHE SAYS INSTEAD
भर्ती मरीज़ों की जानकारी यहाँ नहीं मिलती। आप वार्ड के काउंटर पर जाइए, पहचान-पत्र लेकर — वहाँ से मिलने का पास बनता है।
“We don't give information about admitted patients here. Go to the ward desk with your ID — the visitor pass is issued there.”
It is a real, walkable next step rather than a refusal, and it moves the decision to the desk that can see the patient, ask them, and check a relationship. Ekta can do none of those three things from the lobby.
3
This seat can destroy the access log by working normally
The most consequential finding on this canvas, and it is invisible until the seat has been live for a month.
How the log is built
Every PHI read is stamped with a care context. careContextFor returns treating for the doctor on the encounter, serving for anyone whose patient has a live visit today, and none for everybody else. The file says outright why: “`none` stays rare enough to mean something — nobody at this hospital is looking after this patient today, and somebody opened their chart anyway.”
What this seat does to it
A person off the street asking for their own UHID has no live visit — that is why they are at the enquiry desk. So every lookup here returns none. Forty an hour, six days a week, from one lobby terminal, is more none rows than the rest of the hospital produces put together. The review worklist fills with blameless noise and the one signal it exists for is gone.
DECIDED A directory read is not a chart read, and it gets its own surface
What it returns
Name, age, sex, UHID, and one boolean: is there a visit today. Nothing that varies by encounter. Nothing clinical. Nothing financial.
How it is logged
Its own PhiSurface — directory.lookup — reviewed on its own volume curve, so it never lands in the same worklist as somebody opening a chart.
Why not just narrow the permissions
Because a permission-narrowed chart read is still a chart read in the log. The distinction that matters to a reviewer is what kind of question was asked, and only a separate surface records that.
NEVER, TO ANYONE, ON ANY CHANNEL not a judgement call, not a supervisor override — there is no path through this desk to any of it

Six of these nine are impossible from this seat because the data never reaches the screen — that is the design doing the work. The other three — the cost, the doctor's quality, the room by four o'clock — are things she could say from general knowledge, so they are the three printed under DO NOT ADD on the answer card itself, next to the sentence she is about to read out.
