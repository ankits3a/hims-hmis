What people actually ask

The console on the next page was drawn around one question — is the doctor in — and everything else was inferred. This sheet is the inventory it should have been built from. It is a reconstruction, not a measurement: the ranking is argued, not counted, and the last block says how to settle it for the price of a clipboard.

1
Five different things are all called “an enquiry”, and only one of them is a search
The current console gives all five the same treatment: type, read a card, translate it into a sentence. That is why the commonest question costs the same effort as the rarest.
WALL
The answer never changes
Timings, fees, do you do dialysis, visiting hours, where is the pharmacy.
Zero keystrokes. It is a wall, not a query.
LIVE
True only today
Is Dr Rao in, how long, which beds are free, is the CT working.
The only shape the console currently serves.
JUDGE
Needs a decision, not a fact
Which doctor for this problem, will Ayushman cover it, what will it all cost.
Slowest, most often wrong, costs a whole day.
ROUTE
Somebody else's to answer
Admitted patients, certificates, college admission, jobs, blood.
A named person, never a counter number.
REFUSE
There is no version she may answer
Diagnoses, results, is-he-alive, sex determination, clinical advice.
Scripted, so she is never caught composing one.
And a sixth thing that is not a question at all. A complaint, a man who cannot find his son, a woman crying at the counter, somebody who has been sent in a circle three times. The screen owes these a response, not a lookup — and they are the ones a search box makes worse.

Repeat asks are a different bug
The same person asking “how long now?” three times is a display-board failure, not enquiry volume. Counted separately it is the cheapest evidence that the hall board is not working.
Some questions are signage bugs
Two hundred people a day asking where the pharmacy is means a sign to install, not a person to hire. The desk should be the instrument that proves it.
3
One seat, 24×7 — and three genuinely different jobs
The mix rotates through the day. At 3 a.m. the day screen is not merely idle, it is in the way: the four things that matter are buried under a dark OPD ladder.
06:00 – 17:00 · OPD
the volume, and the peak is 08:00–11:00
Registration, which doctor, where do I go, what is my token, how long, is Ayushman accepted, is the report ready. Plus the entire college lane — admissions, reps, vendors, job seekers — on top of it.
Design problem: a pharma rep and a man carrying his father are both “the next person”. The institution lane has to be parkable and visibly not clinical.
17:00 – 21:00 · Visiting
the traffic turns around and walks upstairs
Visiting hours, passes, which ward, where do I wait, can I stay the night, evening casualty, reports collected late, and the first wave of “nobody has told us anything all day”.
Design problem: almost every question is now about an admitted person and belongs at the IPD counter. She is a router for four hours, and the bounce is the whole risk.
21:00 – 06:00 · Night
small volume, inverted stakes, undesigned everywhere
Emergency, ambulance in and out, “is there a doctor?”, blood, police, relatives arriving at 2 a.m. because they were telephoned, staff calls, and the drunk-caller traffic every hotline gets.
Design problem: the day screen is actively harmful here. Night is a different screen, not a dimmed one.
THE FOUR THINGS A NIGHT SCREEN IS FOR everything else can be a single key away
1 · Who is on call, right now
“Doctor hain kya?” is the night's commonest question and the system cannot answer it — there is no on-call roster anywhere in the codebase. Tonight she answers it from a paper list taped to the desk, and when it is wrong somebody drives forty kilometres for nothing.
2 · The ambulance, both directions
Requested in by a family at 1 a.m., and sent out by the hospital. She needs to see whether it is at the hospital or on a run, and to take an address without asking a frightened caller to repeat it four times.
3 · “Humko phone aaya tha” — we were told to come now
A family arrives at 2 a.m. having been telephoned, and the first person they meet is Ekta. She may know what has happened before they do. She must not be the one to tell them, she must not lie, and she must get them to the right person inside a minute. That is a designed path with a named person at the end of it — not something a twenty-three-year-old improvises at two in the morning.
4 · She is alone in a lobby
One person, one desk, a public entrance, all night. Security is one key and it does not ask her to be certain first — a false alarm costs nothing and hesitating costs everything. This is infrastructure, not a flourish.
DECIDED
Any call that sounds clinical resolves to “come in” or “call the ambulance” — never to reassurance. She cannot triage and must never be placed where she could discourage somebody from coming. There is no version of this screen on which “it is probably nothing” is available to her.
ASKED HERE, ANSWERED NOWHERE each needs a scripted sentence, because a refusal composed on the spot comes out as either a lie or an insult
A diagnosis or a result — including “is it serious?” and “what does this report mean?”
Whether a named person is here — routed to the IPD counter, never confirmed or denied
“Should I bring him in?” — she cannot triage. The answer is always yes, or the ambulance.
Whether somebody is alive — the 2 a.m. question, and the reason path 3 above exists
Whether a doctor is any good — asked constantly, and always about a colleague
Police, employers, journalists — one button: the Duty Manager. Never her decision.
Sex determination — and it will be asked obliquely, here first.
The enquiry counter is the least official-feeling desk in the building, which is exactly why the question gets tested here before anybody asks it in Radiology. The refusal is mandated by law, the PCPNDT declaration is already being built, and this desk needs the same scripted line and the same poster — so it never depends on Ekta being quick enough to catch what is actually being asked.
6
Everything above is argued. Settling it costs a clipboard and one week.
Thirty tick-boxes, counter and phone in separate columns, one blank line for anything that does not fit. About four seconds of Ekta's day.
What it settles for good
Which six questions deserve to be a permanent wall instead of a search result, and in what order. That is the whole layout argument, decided by counting instead of by taste.
Run it over one full week
Monday and Saturday are different hospitals, and the night shift has to be tallied by whoever is actually on it. A three-day sample will lie about the two categories that matter most.
The blank line is the valuable column
Everything nobody predicted lands there, and after a week it is the shortest, truest list of what this hospital does not tell people.
Design from this sheet now — the shapes and the refusals will not move, whatever the counting says, and those are what the layout hangs on. Let the tally correct the ranking in v2. The one thing it is likely to overturn is the position of cost-and-scheme questions, which every hospital under-counts because they are asked at the counter and answered nowhere.
